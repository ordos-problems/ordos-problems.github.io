Short Statement

What are the optimal regret rates for contextual bandits with knapsack constraints when the policy is allowed only a limited number of switches?

Open Problem

Characterize the minimax regret of contextual bandits with knapsacks under a switching budget, including the correct dependence on horizon, budgets, resource dimension, policy-class complexity, and number of allowed switches.

Background & Context

Bandits with knapsacks capture online allocation with learning and finite resources: each action earns stochastic reward and consumes one or more budgeted resources. Contextual versions are central in pricing, ad allocation, and revenue management because customer covariates change the reward and consumption distributions. Separately, limited-switch models impose a practical operational constraint: prices, assortments, or allocation rules cannot be changed every period. Simchi-Levi, Xu, and Zhao recently give a sharp limited-switch theory for blind network revenue management and bandits with knapsacks, showing a piecewise-constant dependence of regret on the switching budget and the number of resources. A possible research direction is their intersection with contextual BwK. However, combining the frameworks does not uniquely specify a model: the switching convention, context process, and comparator must first be fixed. This entry is provisional, not a verified clean open problem.

What Is Known

Simchi-Levi, Xu, and Zhao prove matching upper and lower bounds for blind network revenue management and bandits with knapsacks under limited switches. Their optimal regret rate is governed by a piecewise-constant function of the switch budget and the number of resource constraints. Agrawal, Devanur, and Li give efficient algorithms for contextual bandits with knapsacks without switching constraints. These results motivate asking whether the noncontextual switch-budget structure survives in a specified contextual model. The search conducted for this audit did not establish a sharp theorem for the combined setting, but that absence is not evidence that every such variant is open. Audit caveat (2026-11-09): changing the realized action and changing a mapping from contexts to actions are not equivalent switching constraints. A fixed mapping can choose different actions on successive contexts. This record needs one explicit switching convention, context process, and benchmark before its combined open-status claim can be considered verified.

Remaining open problem. Characterize the minimax regret of contextual bandits with knapsacks under a switching budget, including the correct dependence on horizon, budgets, resource dimension, policy-class complexity, and number of allowed switches.

The noncontextual limited-switch results and contextual BwK results supply useful starting points, but do not by themselves establish an open problem at their intersection. A fixed context-to-action policy can change realized actions on every round, so counting policy changes and counting action changes yield different feasible sets. Before asserting a remaining minimax gap, specify the context process, switching convention, comparator, resource-feasibility convention, and oracle access, then check the literature for that exact model.
