Short Statement

Determine the exponential growth base of the maximum possible number of stable matchings in an n×nn\times nn×n stable-marriage instance.

Open Problem

Determine the sharp exponential base between the known lower construction and the new upper bound.

Background & Context

Stable matching is usually taught as an algorithmic existence theorem: Gale and Shapley guarantee at least one stable matching and find it efficiently. But a single instance can have many stable matchings, forming a distributive lattice. Counting them is computationally hard, and the extremal question asks how large this lattice can be as a function of market size. Knuth posed this in the 1970s. The problem is mathematically clean: preferences are strict and complete, stability has its standard blocking-pair definition, and the objective is just the largest possible number of stable matchings among all n×nn\times nn×n instances. The answer is known to be exponential, but the base remains unknown despite major progress on both lower and upper bounds.

Statement & Model

For an instance of the stable-marriage problem with nnn men and nnn women, each agent has a strict complete preference order over the opposite side. A perfect matching is stable if there is no blocking pair, meaning no man and woman who both prefer each other to their assigned partners. What Is Known

Irving and Leather proved that counting stable matchings is hard and gave early exponential lower-bound constructions. Later gadget constructions give lower bounds with base about 2.282.282.28. Before 2018, the best general upper bounds were superexponential of the form 2nlog⁡n−O(n)2^{n\log n-O(n)}2nlogn−O(n). Karlin, Oveis Gharan, and Weber gave the first simply exponential upper bound, proving f(n)≤cnf(n)\le c^nf(n)≤cn for a universal constant ccc by reducing to downsets of mixing posets. Palmer and Palvolgyi then improved the upper bound from the large Karlin-Oveis Gharan-Weber constant to 3.55n+O(1)3.55^n+O(1)3.55n+O(1) using an entropy formulation. This was the prior exponential gap. Ghorbani and Mnich, 'A 2.81^n upper bound on the number of stable matchings' (arXiv:2611.06494v1, November 4, 2026), Theorem 1, improve the upper bound to f(n)<2.81nf(n)<2.81^nf(n)<2.81n for strict complete preferences. The lower bound is more precisely 2.28n/poly(n)2.28^n/\mathrm{poly}(n)2.28n/poly(n) for all n. The exact exponential base is not determined.

Remaining open problem. Determine the sharp exponential base between the known lower construction and the new upper bound.
