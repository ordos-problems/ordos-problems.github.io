Short Statement

How much does the cardinality budget affect learning a monotone submodular objective from noisy set-value observations? The relevant benchmark here is robust greedy regret, not ordinary approximation regret.

Open Problem

Close the cardinality-dependence gap for unrestricted algorithms under the robust-greedy regret benchmark defined above.

Background & Context

With aggregate feedback, a learner sees only a noisy value of the entire selected set. It cannot directly estimate each item's contribution from that observation. This makes the interaction between set size and exploration informative. A benchmark must also account for the difficulty of identifying a particular greedy trajectory when marginal gains are nearly tied. The earlier description conflated two different comparators. This matters for correctness: an upper bound for a stronger comparator can imply one for a weaker comparator, but a lower bound cannot be transferred in that direction. A purported improvement must therefore first identify the precise objective it controls.

Their lower bound does not thereby become a lower bound for the latter. The stronger cardinality lower bound assumes nonadaptive greedy-error thresholds; Sub-UCB attains the corresponding upper bound. Zhou, Gaillard, Rahier, and Arbel (ALT 2025) study unconstrained nonmonotone functions with a 1/21/21/2 comparator. Nie, Aggarwal, and Quinn study multi-label kkk-submodularity; that kkk is not this cardinality budget. Neither result directly settles this entry.
