Open Problem

Determine the optimal polynomial-time approximation ratio for graph Steiner tree and the exact integrality gap of the hypergraphic relaxation.

Background & Context

Graph Steiner tree asks for the cheapest connected subgraph spanning a specified terminal set and is a foundational network-design problem. The approximation landscape has an unusually large gap: sophisticated LP rounding gives a ratio of ln⁡4≈1.386\ln 4\approx1.386ln4≈1.386, while NP-hardness only rules out ratios below 96/9596/9596/95. The best algorithm uses the hypergraphic relaxation and iterative randomized rounding, which captures full components connecting terminals through Steiner vertices. Despite the strength of this relaxation, known lower bounds for its integrality gap are much smaller than ln⁡4\ln4ln4. The open problem is therefore both algorithmic and polyhedral: identify the true approximation threshold for Steiner tree and the true power of the hypergraphic LP.

What Is Known

Byrka, Grandoni, Rothvoss, and Sanita give a (ln⁡4+ϵ)(\ln4+\epsilon)(ln4+ϵ)-approximation via iterative randomized rounding of the hypergraphic relaxation, and Goemans, Olver, Rothvoss, and Zenklusen later gave a deterministic analysis proving a matching ln⁡4\ln4ln4 upper bound on the hypergraphic integrality gap. Chlebik and Chlebikova prove NP-hardness of approximation below 96/9596/9596/95. Known lower-bound instances for the hypergraphic relaxation give a gap at least 8/78/78/7, far below ln⁡4\ln4ln4. Recent work has improved the related bidirected-cut relaxation below 222, but that does not resolve the stronger hypergraphic relaxation or the true approximation threshold. Thus algorithmic, LP-gap, and hardness bounds remain widely separated.

The hypergraphic relaxation was a major advance because it reasons about full components rather than individual edges, enabling iterative randomized rounding below earlier combinatorial ratios. The remaining gap may be due to a loose analysis of that rounding, an actual gap in the relaxation, or a much stronger hardness construction still unknown. Bidirected-cut developments are related but not identical: BCR is compact and practical, while hypergraphic relaxations are stronger but component-heavy. A resolution likely needs either a new rounding theorem for components or a better understanding of hard instances for terminal-connectivity relaxations.
