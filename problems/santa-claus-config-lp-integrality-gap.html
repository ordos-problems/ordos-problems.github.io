Background & Context

Restricted Santa Claus, also called restricted max-min fair allocation, is the max-min analogue of scheduling and fair division. There are players and indivisible resources; each resource has a value and is acceptable only to some players. The goal is to allocate resources so that the least-valued player bundle is as large as possible. The configuration LP is the standard relaxation: for a guessed target value TTT, each player may fractionally choose configurations of resources whose total value is at least TTT. The LP is strong enough to certify constant-factor existence results, but rounding it constructively is difficult because feasible configurations form a hypergraph matching problem. The exact integrality gap of this LP is a central structural question: it measures the true power of the canonical relaxation independently of the current limitations of algorithms.

What Is Known

Bansal and Sviridenko introduced the configuration-LP approach for Santa Claus-type allocation and showed why weaker assignment relaxations are inadequate. Asadpour, Feige, and Saberi proved that the restricted configuration LP has constant integrality gap at most 444 using hypergraph matching and local-search ideas, but their existence proof did not by itself yield a polynomial-time 444-approximation. Jansen and Rohwedder gave a shorter analysis improving the upper bound to 3+5/63+5/63+5/6, and Cheng and Mao later improved it to 3+21/26≈3.8083+21/26\approx 3.8083+21/26≈3.808 while also developing stronger constructive algorithms. On the lower-bound side, explicit instances show an integrality gap of at least 222. The remaining gap interval is [2,53/15][2,53/15][2,53/15]. Haxell and Szabo (SODA 2023; Combinatorica 2025), Theorem 1.1, improve the restricted configuration-LP gap to 53/1553/1553/15. This is existential, not a polynomial-time allocation algorithm.

The restricted Santa Claus literature separates three questions: estimating the optimum via the configuration LP, proving existential integrality-gap bounds, and finding polynomial-time algorithms that construct allocations. The configuration LP is the natural relaxation for the first two questions, but its rounding is tied to difficult hypergraph matching local search. The historical 444 and 3.8083.8083.808 upper bounds, now improved to 53/1553/1553/15, show substantial structure, while the lower bound 222 leaves room for either sharper rounding or stronger gap constructions.
