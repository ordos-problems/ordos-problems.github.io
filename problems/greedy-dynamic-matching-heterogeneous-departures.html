Short Statement

In dynamic matching with Poisson arrivals, exponential abandonment and rewards, does the LP-selected greedy policy of Arnosti and Simon always achieve the tight 1/21/21/2 ratio against the omniscient benchmark without any homogeneous-departure-rate assumption?

Open Problem

Prove or refute that the Arnosti-Simon LP-selected greedy policy achieves at least 1/21/21/2 of the omniscient benchmark for every finite dynamic matching instance with arbitrary heterogeneous exponential departure rates and rewards.

Background & Context

Dynamic matching with abandonment is a clean model for markets where compatibility is valuable but delay risks losing agents: kidney exchange pools, labor platforms, freight exchanges, rideshare pooling, and peer-to-peer barter markets all have this shape. A planner sees agents arrive over time, can match compatible agents currently in the system, and loses agents who depart unmatched. The fundamental tension is whether to match greedily or wait for market thickness. In full generality, the offline optimum is too strong because it knows future arrivals and departure times, but it is a useful benchmark for competitive analysis. The Arnosti-Simon paper is important because it asks a very sharp question inside this fixed stochastic model: can a deterministic greedy policy, chosen by an LP, always get the optimal possible 1/21/21/2 of the omniscient benchmark? They prove this in equal-departure and certain bipartite settings, and no online policy can beat 1/21/21/2 there. The missing theorem is exactly the kind this site should track: extend or refute the 1/21/21/2 guarantee for heterogeneous departure rates.

What Is Known

Collina, Immorlica, Leyton-Brown, Lucier, and Newman proved a 1/81/81/8-competitive algorithm for dynamic weighted matching with heterogeneous arrivals and departures. Aouad and Saritac developed an LP-based approach for dynamic stochastic matching under limited time in Operations Research. Arnosti and Simon introduce LPALGLP^{ALG}LPALG to select a greedy policy and prove that its value lower-bounds the greedy policy in two main cases: equal departure rates for all types, and bipartite instances with equal departure rates on each side. They also prove LPALGLP^{ALG}LPALG is at least half of the omniscient benchmark, yielding a tight 1/21/21/2 guarantee in those settings, and show no online policy can do better than 1/21/21/2. They prove the heterogeneous-departure conjecture for two types and report no computational counterexample, but the arbitrary heterogeneous-rate case remains open.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The 2025 Arnosti-Simon paper improves the general state of knowledge from a 1/81/81/8 guarantee to a tight 1/21/21/2 theorem in homogeneous-departure settings and proves the conjecture for two types. The heterogeneous many-type case is the remaining gap. Arnosti and Simon, Greedy Dynamic Matching Collina et al., Dynamic Weighted Matching with Heterogeneous Arrival and Departure Rates Aouad and Saritac, Dynamic Stochastic Matching Under Limited Time

Earlier dynamic matching theory established that greedy and patient policies can be dramatically different depending on information and market thickness, but heterogeneous weighted models resisted sharp guarantees. Collina et al. handled arbitrary heterogeneous arrival and departure rates with a 1/81/81/8 competitive ratio against an omniscient benchmark. Aouad and Saritac gave an Operations Research LP framework for limited-time dynamic stochastic matching. Arnosti and Simon sharpen the model by studying deterministic greedy policies selected through LPALGLP^{ALG}LPALG and prove the best possible 1/21/21/2 ratio in homogeneous-departure cases. Their proof hinges on a lemma relating the probability that some agent in a set is present to the expected number of such agents. The open problem is whether an analogous inequality or replacement argument survives heterogeneous departure rates, where fast-departing and slow-departing types create asymmetric inventory effects.
