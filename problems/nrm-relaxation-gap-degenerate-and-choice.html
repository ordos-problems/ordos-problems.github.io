Short Statement

In network revenue management, the deterministic LP upper-bounds optimal revenue and re-solving heuristics achieve O(1)O(1)O(1) regret under non-degeneracy in the independent-demand case. For choice-based NRM, no policy is known to achieve constant (TTT-independent) regret without a non-degeneracy assumption, and the tight relaxation gap and best simple-policy regret are uncharacterized.

Open Problem

Find a tractable policy with O(1)O(1)O(1) regret for general choice-based network revenue management, including degenerate CDLP instances, or prove that such uniformly bounded regret is impossible.

Background & Context

Network revenue management is a foundational model for selling products that consume shared limited resources, such as airline itineraries, hotel stays, or ad inventory. The exact dynamic program is usually intractable, so much of the theory compares online policies against deterministic linear-program relaxations and asks for small regret as the horizon and capacities scale. In quantity-based NRM with accept/reject decisions, re-solving and bid-price policies now have strong guarantees, including uniformly bounded loss in important nondegenerate settings. Degeneracy matters because the deterministic LP can have multiple optimal bases or flat directions; then small demand noise can change the right acceptance mix. Choice-based NRM adds another difficulty: the decision is an assortment or offer set, and customers choose according to a demand model, so the CDLP may be nonlinear or have a more complicated feasible structure. The open problem is clean: obtain O(1)O(1)O(1) regret, or prove it impossible, for general choice-based NRM including degenerate instances.

What Is Known

Talluri and van Ryzin introduced deterministic LP and randomized linear-programming methods for NRM. Liu and van Ryzin developed the choice-based deterministic linear program for assortment-based revenue management. Bumpensanti and Wang prove that for canonical quantity-based NRM, a carefully timed re-solving heuristic with probability thresholds has uniformly bounded loss independent of horizon and capacities. Jiang, Ma, and Zhang show that degeneracy is not fatal in a continuous-value quantity-based NRM model: they obtain O(log⁡2T)O(\log^2 T)O(log2T) regret without nondegeneracy assumptions and O(log⁡T)O(\log T)O(logT) under second-order growth. These results do not yet give a general O(1)O(1)O(1) regret theorem for choice-based NRM under degenerate CDLP structure.

Remaining open problem. Find a tractable policy with O(1)O(1)O(1) regret for general choice-based network revenue management, including degenerate CDLP instances, or prove that such uniformly bounded regret is impossible.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Quantity-based NRM has bounded-loss and logarithmic-regret guarantees in increasingly broad settings. General choice-based NRM with degenerate CDLP remains the unresolved frontier. Bumpensanti and Wang, A Re-solving Heuristic with Uniformly Bounded Loss Jiang, Ma, Zhang, Degeneracy is OK Liu and van Ryzin, On the choice-based linear programming model for network revenue management

The NRM literature has made sharp progress by understanding when deterministic relaxations are accurate enough for online control. Bumpensanti and Wang show that naive frequent re-solving is not enough, but a sparse re-solving schedule plus thresholding can obtain bounded loss in canonical NRM. Jiang, Ma, and Zhang weaken nondegeneracy requirements for continuous-value quantity-based NRM and introduce tools such as semi-fluid relaxations and dual-convergence bounds. Choice-based NRM is structurally harder because decisions alter a customer's substitution set rather than accept or reject a fixed request, and CDLP degeneracy can interact with substitution in ways not covered by quantity-based analyses. The live problem is to decide whether the bounded-loss paradigm survives this combination.
