Short Statement

For i.i.d. first-passage percolation on Zd\mathbb Z^dZd, prove a polynomial sublinear variance bound, or determine the true fluctuation exponent.

Open Problem

The open problem is to determine the correct fluctuation scale.

Background & Context

First-passage percolation is one of the central random optimization models in probability. It turns a lattice into a random metric space and asks for the fastest route through a random medium. The model has direct OR intuition: it is a stochastic shortest-path problem on a huge random network. The shape theorem describes the deterministic large-scale growth ball, but the second-order behavior is much harder. In exactly solvable directed last-passage models, KPZ theory predicts and often proves 1/31/31/3 passage-time fluctuations and 2/32/32/3 transversal fluctuations. Undirected lattice FPP is not integrable, so even proving any polynomial improvement over linear variance has resisted the standard concentration and martingale methods. This makes the problem a clean test of whether KPZ-type behavior can be proved without exact formulas.

What Is Known

Kesten proved foundational concentration and linear-variance-type bounds. Benjamini, Kalai, and Schramm used hypercontractivity to improve the variance upper bound to O(n/log⁡n)O(n/\log n)O(n/logn) for certain two-valued passage-time distributions, and later work extended sublinear variance bounds under broader assumptions. The Auffinger-Damron-Hanson survey records the state of the art and many related open questions on geodesics, Busemann functions, and fluctuations. For exactly solvable directed last-passage percolation models, KPZ exponents are known in many cases, but these techniques do not transfer to undirected i.i.d. FPP. In 2026, Basu, Sidoravicius, and Sly proved a polynomial variance improvement for a rotationally invariant Riemannian FPP model, breaking the n/log⁡nn/\log nn/logn barrier in a continuous setting. That result is strong evidence for the right phenomenon, but it does not resolve the classical lattice FPP variance problem.

The variance problem has evolved through concentration inequalities, influence bounds, and geodesic geometry. Kesten's work gave the basic quantitative framework. Benjamini, Kalai, and Schramm introduced a hypercontractive method showing that individual edges have small influence, yielding n/log⁡nn/\log nn/logn-scale variance bounds in important cases. Later work generalized this sublinear improvement, but the logarithmic saving remains far from the KPZ prediction. The Auffinger-Damron-Hanson survey places variance among a web of open problems involving geodesics, Busemann functions, limit-shape curvature, and competition interfaces. Basu-Sidoravicius-Sly's rotationally invariant result suggests that disorder chaos and multiscale geodesic decorrelation can produce polynomial savings, but the lattice model lacks the same symmetry and currently has no comparable theorem.
