Short Statement

For additive valuations over indivisible goods, characterize when competitive equilibria exist for almost every income vector.

Open Problem

More generally, determine for which pairs (n,m)(n,m)(n,m) and valuation classes additive competitive equilibrium exists for almost all incomes, and for which pairs nonexistence persists on a positive-measure set.

Background & Context

Competitive equilibrium is one of the cleanest bridges between economics and optimization. With divisible goods and convex preferences, prices decentralize efficient allocations and support fairness properties. Indivisible goods break this theory: exact market-clearing prices may not exist because an agent's demand jumps discontinuously as prices or budgets vary. Generic budgets are a natural repair. Instead of insisting on equal artificial incomes, perturb the income vector and ask whether the nonexistence pathologies are lower-dimensional coincidences. This idea is especially relevant to fair allocation mechanisms such as course allocation, public resource assignment, and allocation with unequal entitlements. The additive-valuation case is the most parsimonious indivisible-goods model: values are fully specified by item-agent weights, with no complementarities. If generic budgets do not restore equilibrium here, the obstruction is fundamental rather than a modeling artifact.

What Is Known

Babaioff, Nisan, and Talgam-Cohen showed positive results for two agents with equal budgets after generic perturbation, and for several non-equal-budget cases such as identical preferences. Segal-Halevi completed the characterization for monotonically increasing preferences in several small cases left open by the earlier paper: for four goods and three or four agents with general monotone preferences, almost-everywhere equilibrium may fail; for four goods and three additive agents, existence holds. Segal-Halevi also characterized monotone decreasing preferences for chores and proved fairness properties of competitive-equilibrium allocations. The additive-goods frontier remains unresolved. In particular, the broad conjecture that two agents with additive valuations admit competitive equilibrium for almost every income vector and any number of goods is not settled by the known small-mmm and monotone-preference classifications.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Several small cases and broad monotone-preference cases are classified, but the additive frontier remains open, including the natural two-agent additive case for arbitrary numbers of goods. Babaioff-Nisan-Talgam-Cohen generic budgets Segal-Halevi almost all incomes

The generic-budget literature asks whether indivisibility failures are knife-edge equal-income artifacts. Babaioff, Nisan, and Talgam-Cohen establish the framework and prove important positive cases. Segal-Halevi then resolves several monotone-preference cases, showing both positive and negative phenomena and introducing a sequential-game view of equilibrium. The additive setting remains appealing because additive valuations are expressive enough for many assignment markets but avoid complementarities. The remaining gap is a structural characterization: either generic incomes restore equilibrium for broad additive domains, or there are additive nonexistence regions that have not yet been discovered. A solution may require combining polyhedral demand geometry, hyperplane-arrangement arguments, and explicit finite counterexamples.
