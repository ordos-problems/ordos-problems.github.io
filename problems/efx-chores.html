Short Statement

For indivisible chores (which impose costs), an EFX allocation is one where any envy agent iii feels toward jjj vanishes after dropping any single chore from iii's own bundle. Does an EFX allocation always exist for additive chores with three agents?

Open Problem

The precise remaining gap: does an EFX allocation always exist for chores with (unrestricted) additive costs and exactly n=3n=3n=3 agents?

Background & Context

Once envy-freeness up to any good (EFX) was proposed for indivisible goods, it was natural to ask about its mirror image for chores — indivisible tasks or costs that agents dislike rather than desire, such as household duties, unwanted job assignments, or waste-disposal responsibilities. The chores setting is not a trivial relabeling of the goods setting: negating valuations flips incentives in ways that break many of the arguments that work for goods, and indeed the two settings have turned out to have surprisingly different answers. For goods, no EFX nonexistence example is known for additive valuations at any number of agents; for chores, by contrast, nonexistence had long been suspected and was confirmed for small cases, revealing a genuine asymmetry between the two models that is itself an interesting structural finding in fair division theory. This makes the chores side of EFX a natural stress-test for which techniques from the goods literature (envy-cycle elimination, rainbow cycle numbers, champion graphs) transfer and which break down, and why. Beyond its theoretical interest, chore division is arguably the more common real-world scenario — splitting caregiving duties, unpleasant shifts, or cost-sharing obligations among roommates, partners, or coworkers — so pinning down exactly where EFX allocations do and do not exist for chores has direct implications for designing fair task-assignment mechanisms. The problem also serves as a proving ground for the interaction between fairness and efficiency (Pareto-optimality), since a recurring theme in the chores literature is that EFX and Pareto-optimality can conflict even when EFX by itself is achievable, unlike in most known goods settings.

What Is Known

The general chores analogue of the EFX-for-goods conjecture is now FALSE: He and Tao (arXiv, June 2026) prove that even for tri-valued additive cost functions (only three distinct positive cost levels), for every n≥4n\ge4n≥4 there is an instance with no EFX allocation; since tri-valued costs are additive costs, this resolves the n≥4n\ge4n≥4 general-additive-chores existence question negatively. Their construction is tight: EFX is known to always exist for chores with at most two distinct cost values (bi-valued), so three values is the minimum needed for a counterexample. They further show that even for bi-valued instances, EFX can be incompatible with Pareto-optimality once n≥4n\ge4n≥4 (the first such example with strictly positive costs), while an EFX allocation itself is guaranteed to exist for every bi-valued 4-agent instance. Earlier, Christoforidis and Santorinaios (IJCAI 2024) had shown EFX can fail to exist for only n=3n=3n=3 agents under SUPERADDITIVE cost functions (six chores suffice), establishing the first goods/chores separation and NP-completeness of EFX-existence-checking for general monotone costs, but that result does not apply to additive costs. For (unrestricted) additive costs specifically, positive existence results hold when the number of chores is at most n+2n+2n+2, when all but one agent share an identical ordering over chores, and (via Kobayashi et al. 2025 and Garg et al. 2023) for three-agent personalized bi-valued costs. In general, a 2-EFX allocation (envy vanishes after removing the two costliest chores) always exists for additive chores (Zhou, Wu 2022/2024), and this approximation factor has since been sharpened further by several 2024-2026 papers (e.g., 'Existence of 2-EFX Allocations of Chores', 2025; 'Constant-Factor EFX Exists for Chores', 2024). As of July 2026, the single remaining open case that gives this problem its name — complete EFX existence for additive costs with exactly n=3n=3n=3 agents — has NOT been resolved by the He-Tao nonexistence result (which requires n≥4n\ge4n≥4) and remains open.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Major 2026 development: He and Tao (arXiv 2606.08872, submitted 7 June 2026) proved that EFX allocations can fail to exist for additive chores whenever n≥4n\ge4n≥4, using a tri-valued (hence additive) counterexample, and additionally proved every EFX allocation can fail to be Pareto-optimal for bi-valued chores when n≥4n\ge4n≥4. This settles the general-nnn question negatively but their construction explicitly requires n≥4n\ge4n≥4; the specific n=3n=3n=3, fully general additive-costs case (the version this problem entry is about) is NOT addressed by their counterexample and remains open as of July 2026. Positive existence for the n=4n=4n=4 bi-valued special case was also newly shown by the same paper. No paper found claims to resolve n=3n=3n=3 additive chores either way. The chores side of EFX has followed a 'divide and conquer by number of distinct cost values' pattern: bi-valued instances are well understood (existence holds quite broadly, including for n=3n=3n=3 personalized bi-valued costs per Garg et al. 2023 and Kobayashi et al. 2025, and for n=4n=4n=4 per He-Tao 2026), while tri-valued and general additive instances are where the picture darkens. Christoforidis and Santorinaios (IJCAI 2024) first showed a goods/chores asymmetry by exhibiting nonexistence for n=3n=3n=3 under superadditive costs and proving NP-completeness of EFX-checking for monotone costs, but left the additive case open. The 2026 breakthrough by He and Tao closes the general-nnn additive question by constructing a tri-valued (hence additive), n≥4n\ge4n≥4 nonexistence example — tight in the sense that only two distinct cost values would not suffice — and separately shows EFX and Pareto-optimality can conflict for bi-valued costs once n≥4n\ge4n≥4, extending known EFX/PO incompatibility results (which previously needed zero-cost items) to strictly positive costs. Crucially, their counterexample construction inherently needs at least four agents; nothing in the current literature settles whether n=3n=3n=3, unrestricted-additive-cost EFX allocations always exist. This leaves n=3n=3n=3 as a sharply delineated remaining gap: below it (bi-valued or n≤2n\le2n≤2) existence is known, and just above it (tri-valued, n≥4n\ge4n≥4) nonexistence is now known, so n=3n=3n=3 general additive costs is the precise unresolved boundary case. In parallel, the approximation literature has matured quickly, with 2-EFX (Zhou-Wu) and various constant-factor / conditional bounds (2024-2026) narrowing the practical gap even while exact existence remains unsettled for the hardest cases.

We show that, even for tri-valued additive cost functions, for every n≥4, there exists an instance with n agents where no EFX allocation exists.
