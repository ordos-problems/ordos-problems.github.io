Short Statement

For three agents dividing indivisible chores with additive costs, complete EFX is known for at most eight chores. The arbitrary-size existence question remains.

Open Problem

Does every three-agent instance with nonnegative additive chore costs admit a complete exact EFX allocation, for arbitrarily many chores?

Background & Context

For chores, the envious agent hypothetically removes a task from its own bundle; for goods, removal is from the envied bundle. Negating utilities therefore does not simply transfer the known goods proofs. With additive costs, higher-agent counterexamples now rule out a universal existence theorem, while three-agent existence remains an important separate target. Recent bounded-size existence results, approximate fairness results, and incompatibility with Pareto efficiency each answer different questions. This entry fixes three agents, additive nonnegative costs, complete allocation, and exact EFX, so that progress can be compared within one model.

What Is Known

Wentao He and Biaoshuai Tao (arXiv:2606.08872v2, July 2026), Theorems 1-3, give additive counterexamples for every n≥4n\ge4n≥4, using three positive cost values. They also prove four-agent bi-valued EFX existence and bi-valued EFX/Pareto-optimality incompatibility for n≥4n\ge4n≥4. This is not a bi-valued existence theorem for all nnn. Xinkai Zhang, 'EFX Allocations for Three Agents and Seven or Eight Chores' (arXiv:2609.10585v2, October 2026), Theorems 1.1-1.2, handles seven and eight chores with arbitrary nonnegative additive costs. Together with the earlier m≤2nm\le2nm≤2n theorem, this covers three-agent instances with at most eight chores. The computer-assisted certificates were not replayed here. Jugal Garg and Aniket Murhekar (arXiv:2507.19461v1) prove existence of multiplicative 2-EFX for any number of additive agents: after deleting any one owned chore, its remaining cost is at most twice the comparison bundle's cost. This does not mean deleting two chores. Remaining open problem. Prove complete exact EFX for three additive agents without bounding the number of chores, or give a counterexample.

“We consider the fair division problem of indivisible chores and resolve the long-standing open problem for the existence of EFX allocations with additive cost functions. We show that, even for tri-valued additive cost functions, for every n≥4, there exists an instance with n agents where no EFX allocation exists. Our counterexample only uses three types of chores, which is also tight on the number of types, as an EFX allocation is known to exist for two types of chores.”— Abstract
