Short Statement

New matrix classifications make the BAR tightness condition easier to verify. Do those algebraic conditions also force prelimit stability and the stronger steady-state state-space-collapse condition?

Open Problem

Does the stated BAR tight-system condition imply prelimit stability and uniform integrability of high-priority stationary queue lengths under the primitive and heavy-traffic assumptions? Give a general implication with explicit regularity assumptions or a counterexample. The 2025 matrix classifications alone do not settle this.

Background & Context

Heavy-traffic theory for multiclass queueing networks is one of the main ways applied probability turns complex service systems into tractable diffusion approximations. The hard part is usually steady state: proving convergence of stationary distributions requires stability, tightness, and interchange of limits, not just process-level weak convergence from a fixed initial condition. The BAR approach attacks the stationary equation directly and has produced sharp results for generalized Jackson networks. Extending it to multiclass networks under static-buffer-priority service is important because priority disciplines are common in manufacturing, healthcare, communication systems, and computing. Braverman, Dai, and Miyazawa isolate three verifiable modules. If one of them, the tight-matrix condition, automatically implied the others, the method would become much more powerful and easier to apply.

What Is Known

Braverman, Dai, and Miyazawa's published Stochastic Systems 15(1):1-49 paper (2025), Theorem 5.1, proves steady-state diffusion convergence under separate primitive-moment, heavy-traffic, stability, uniform-integrability, and tight-system assumptions, with positive-definite limiting covariance. The paragraph following Corollary 5.1 explicitly leaves the relation between stability, SSC, and tightness open beyond the studied examples. Dai, Ji, and Miyazawa (arXiv:2404.13651v3, July 2025), Theorem 3.1, classify tight completely-S\mathcal SS matrices in dimension two; Theorem 3.2 gives a sufficient higher-dimensional condition. Corollary 4.2 shows that a well-defined completely-S\mathcal SS reflection matrix arising from a two-station SBP network is tight. Theorem 4.1 covers LBFS reentrant lines. Their three-station, seven-class FBFS example has a non-tight matrix; it does not refute the implication from tightness to stability or SSC. Remaining open problem. Derive the prelimit stability and uniform-integrability assumptions from the algebraic condition, or show precisely why they are independent. Verifying a matrix condition is not itself that implication.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Published 2025 BAR results and a revised 2025 tight-matrix classification clarify the assumptions and settle verification in important cases. The general implication to stability and SSC remains distinct. Published BAR theorem and open relationship (2025) J. G. Dai, Yiquan Ji, Masakiyo Miyazawa (2025)

The BAR convergence theorem assumes probabilistic control of the prelimit system as well as algebraic control of its limiting reflection data. Recent work makes the algebraic part concrete, especially for two stations and LBFS reentrant lines. It does not remove the separate need for stationary laws or the uniform integrability of high-priority counts. In particular, a stable FBFS example with a non-tight matrix concerns failure of automatic tightness, not a counterexample in the direction asked here. The entry now states the actual strong SSC assumption instead of equating it with a scaled weak limit.
