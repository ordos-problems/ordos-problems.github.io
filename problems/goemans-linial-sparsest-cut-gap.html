Short Statement

Determine the asymptotic integrality gap of the Goemans-Linial semidefinite relaxation for uniform Sparsest Cut.

Open Problem

The open problem is to determine this gap up to constants or sharp asymptotics.

Background & Context

Sparsest Cut is a central graph-partitioning problem with applications in clustering, VLSI, divide-and-conquer graph algorithms, metric embeddings, and flow-cut gaps. The Goemans-Linial SDP was one of the major semidefinite relaxations in approximation algorithms. Arora, Rao, and Vazirani's rounding breakthrough gave an O(log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n})O(logn​) approximation for Sparsest Cut and Balanced Separator and made the geometry of negative-type metrics a central object. Understanding the exact performance of this SDP would clarify whether the ARV rounding paradigm is close to optimal or whether a better geometric theorem exists. The problem is clean: the relaxation is fixed, the objective is fixed, and both upper and lower bounds are mathematical statements about finite metrics and graph cuts.

The Goemans-Linial semidefinite relaxation assigns vectors to vertices and enforces squared Euclidean distances satisfying triangle inequalities, producing an SDP value that lower-bounds the optimum cut sparsity. Its integrality gap is the worst-case ratio between the true optimum and the SDP optimum over nnn-vertex instances. The open problem is to determine this gap up to constants or sharp asymptotics. What Is Known

The ARV framework gives an O(log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n})O(logn​)-type upper bound for rounding the SDP. Earlier lower bounds showed an Ω(log⁡log⁡n)\Omega(\log\log n)Ω(loglogn) integrality gap. Kane and Meka used pseudorandom generators for Lipschitz functions of low-degree polynomials to construct uniform Sparsest Cut instances with Goemans-Linial SDP gap exp⁡(Ω((log⁡log⁡n)1/2))\exp(\Omega((\log\log n)^{1/2}))exp(Ω((loglogn)1/2)), a near-exponential improvement over the previous lower bound in the iterated-log scale. This still leaves a large gap to the O(log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n})O(logn​) upper bound. Related work on metric embeddings and Unique Games explains why some stronger approximation goals are hard, but the precise integrality gap of this particular SDP in the uniform setting remains unresolved.

The Goemans-Linial SDP gap problem sits at the intersection of approximation algorithms and metric geometry. ARV-style rounding gives the principal upper bound by showing that negative-type metrics contain useful separated sets. Lower bounds construct negative-type metrics that are hard to embed into L1L_1L1​, then realize or relate them to cut instances.
