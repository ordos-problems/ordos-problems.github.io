Background & Context

Job-shop scheduling is one of the canonical hard models in operations research. Each job has its own ordered route through machines, and the scheduler must interleave all routes while respecting both machine capacity and within-job precedence. The model captures a standard manufacturing abstraction, but the open question here is purely algorithmic: the routes, processing times, and makespan objective are already fully specified. For a fixed number of machines, approximation schemes are known for open shop and flow shop, and job shop also has approximation schemes when the maximum number of operations in a job is treated as an additional parameter. Mnich and van Bevern isolate the sharper question of whether that route-length parameter is genuinely necessary. A positive answer would say that a small number of machines is enough to obtain a true fixed-parameter approximation scheme for arbitrary job routes; a negative answer would identify a rare barrier for approximation schemes in a fixed-machine shop model.

What Is Known

Three-machine flow shop and three-machine open shop are already strongly NP-hard, so exact polynomial algorithms are not expected even for very small fixed shop environments. Nevertheless, approximation behaves better. Sevastianov and Woeginger give a fixed-parameter approximation scheme for open-shop makespan with dependence f(m,ϵ)+O(nlog⁡n)f(m,\epsilon)+O(n\log n)f(m,ϵ)+O(nlogn), and Hall gives an analogous approximation scheme for flow-shop makespan. For job shop, Jansen, Solis-Oba, and Sviridenko give a (1+ϵ)(1+\epsilon)(1+ϵ) approximation in time depending on mmm, ϵ\epsilonϵ, and nmax⁡n_{\max}nmax​, plus linear or near-linear input dependence. Mnich and van Bevern pose Open Problem 13: remove the dependence on nmax⁡n_{\max}nmax​. The usual route to a negative answer is not straightforward, because showing W-hardness parameterized by Cmax⁡+mC_{\max}+mCmax​+m cannot work: with both Cmax⁡C_{\max}Cmax​ and mmm bounded, the number of operation slots is bounded and the problem is trivially fixed-parameter tractable.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Approximation schemes are known with extra dependence on route length or related job-structure parameters. A machine-only FPT approximation scheme remains open. Mnich and van Bevern, 15 open problems Jansen, Solis-Oba, Sviridenko, job-shop approximation scheme

The positive results for open shop and flow shop show that fixed-machine shop scheduling is not automatically hostile to FPT approximation schemes. The obstruction in job shop is route complexity: a single job may revisit machines many times, so the schedule is not just an ordering of jobs on each machine but an interleaving of many route segments. Existing approximation schemes control this by including nmax⁡n_{\max}nmax​ in the parameter dependence. The open problem asks whether long routes can be compressed after rounding, perhaps by aggregating repeated visits or by proving that only a bounded set of critical operations matters for a (1+ϵ)(1+\epsilon)(1+ϵ) schedule. A lower bound would need a subtler framework than standard EPTAS impossibility through optimum-parameter hardness, because bounded makespan and bounded machines already bound the number of operations that can appear in a yes-instance.
