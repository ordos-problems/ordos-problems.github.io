Short Statement

How many extra i.i.d. bidders does a simple mechanism (selling items separately, or VCG) need to out-earn the optimal mechanism on the original bidders, in multi-item settings? The additive independent case is now settled at Θ(nm)\Theta(\sqrt{nm})Θ(nm​) for m<nm<nm<n; constants, the m≥nm\ge nm≥n regime, non-additive valuations, and correlated items remain open.

Open Problem

(i) the exact constants and the tight bound in the regime m≥nm\ge nm≥n; (ii) competition complexity for non-additive valuations (unit-demand, XOS, subadditive) where no finite bounds or only much weaker bounds are known; (iii) correlated item distributions; (iv) the competition complexity of other simple formats such as grand-bundle auctions beyond the single-bidder case.

Background & Context

Bulow and Klemperer's 1996 theorem is one of the most quoted results in auction theory: for a single item and nnn i.i.d. regular bidders, a simple second-price auction with n+1n+1n+1 bidders earns at least as much revenue as Myerson's optimal auction with nnn bidders. The managerial message — investing in competition beats investing in mechanism-design sophistication — is compelling, but the single-item assumption is restrictive: real sellers (ad exchanges, spectrum auctions, e-commerce platforms) sell many items, and multi-item optimal mechanisms are notoriously complex, randomized, and prior-dependent (they can even require infinite menus). Eden, Feldman, Friedler, Talgam-Cohen, and Weinberg (EC 2017) introduced the competition complexity of an auction environment: the number of extra bidders that simple mechanisms — selling every item separately via second-price auctions (SRev), or running VCG — need in order to beat the optimal Bayesian incentive-compatible mechanism's revenue on the original bidders. This framework converts the vague 'simple vs. optimal' tradeoff into a sharp quantitative question and connects to the duality-based revenue-benchmark machinery of Cai, Devanur, and Weinberg. A decade of work has pinned down the additive/independent case up to constants: Beyhaghi and Weinberg (STOC 2019) proved O(nm)O(\sqrt{nm})O(nm​) (for m<nm<nm<n) and O(nln⁡(m/n))O(n\ln(m/n))O(nln(m/n)) (for m≥nm\ge nm≥n) upper bounds, and Derakhshan, Ryu, Weinberg, and Xue (EC 2024) proved the matching Ω(nm)\Omega(\sqrt{nm})Ω(nm​) lower bound via truncated equal-revenue distributions. What remains open is everything beyond this base case: exact constants, the fine structure of the m≥nm\ge nm≥n regime, non-additive valuations (unit-demand, XOS, subadditive), correlated items, and the power of other simple formats such as grand bundling.

Progress & Literature

The field is organized around the Cai-Devanur-Weinberg duality framework, which upper-bounds optimal multi-item revenue by tractable benchmarks, and progress has consisted of matching those benchmarks with the revenue of simple auctions with extra bidders. Eden, Feldman, Friedler, Talgam-Cohen, Weinberg (EC 2017) proved the first multi-item Bulow-Klemperer results this way (n+2m−2n+2m-2n+2m−2 extra bidders for additive bidders over independent regular items; also a single unit-demand bidder). Feldman, Friedler, Rubinstein (EC 2018) showed O(nln⁡(m/n))O(n\ln(m/n))O(nln(m/n)) extra bidders suffice even for 99%99\%99% of the benchmark, with a matching Ω(nln⁡(m/n))\Omega(n\ln(m/n))Ω(nln(m/n)) necessity under regularity. Beyhaghi and Weinberg (STOC 2019) sharpened the upper bounds to O(nm)O(\sqrt{nm})O(nm​) for m<nm<nm<n, and Derakhshan, Ryu, Weinberg, Xue (EC 2024) closed that regime with an Ω(nm)\Omega(\sqrt{nm})Ω(nm​) lower bound built from an explicit Bayesian IC auction for truncated equal-revenue distributions, also proving these truncations always witness worst-case competition complexity. The 2025 'Beyond VCG and Regularity' paper (Beyhaghi, Cai, Feng, Li, Weinberg) shows the entire mmm-dependence is an artifact of bare regularity: under α\alphaα-strong regularity the answer is Θ(n/α)\Theta(n/\alpha)Θ(n/α), and grand bundling admits constant competition complexity in natural single-bidder cases. The main remaining gaps: no tight answer (even asymptotically, with constants) in the m≥nm\ge nm≥n regime; essentially nothing tight beyond additive valuations — for unit-demand, XOS, or subadditive bidders the duality benchmarks lose constant factors, so new benchmark technology or direct coupling arguments are needed; and correlated items break the per-item second-price analysis entirely.
