Short Statement

For random geometric graphs generated by a smooth kernel on the sphere, prove or refute the conjecture that the detection threshold against Erdos-Renyi is governed by n3tr⁡2(κ3)≍1n^3\operatorname{tr}^2(\kappa^3)\asymp 1n3tr2(κ3)≍1.

Open Problem

Prove this conjecture in general, or identify kernels/scalings for which the spectral trace criterion is not sharp.

Background & Context

Random geometric graphs are basic models for networks with latent spatial, similarity, or feature geometry. A central inference question is whether an observed graph contains latent geometry at all, or whether it is statistically indistinguishable from an Erdos-Renyi graph with the same edge density. This is a clean testing problem with a long line of work in probability, statistics, information theory, and theoretical computer science. Earlier high-dimensional RGG results focused heavily on hard threshold kernels, where edges are created by spherical caps. Smooth kernels are closer to many statistical network models because edge probabilities vary continuously with latent similarity. Mao, Wu, and Xu showed that smoothness can dramatically change the detection scale: in dense graphs, the critical dimension becomes d=n3/4d=n^{3/4}d=n3/4 instead of the much larger hard-threshold scale. Their work suggests that the right general invariant is spectral, not merely dimension or edge density.

What Is Known

Bubeck, Ding, Eldan, and Racz developed efficient tests and lower bounds for high-dimensional geometric graphs with hard threshold kernels and posed sparse-regime detection conjectures. Liu, Mohanty, Schramm, and Yang nearly resolved the sparse hard-kernel conjecture up to logarithmic factors. Mao, Wu, and Xu proved the sharp d=n3/4d=n^{3/4}d=n3/4 detection threshold for dense smooth kernels and extended the analysis to signal-to-noise scalings. They also established an estimation threshold for recovering latent vectors up to rotation. The general spectral trace conjecture n3tr⁡2(κ3)≍1n^3\operatorname{tr}^2(\kappa^3)\asymp 1n3tr2(κ3)≍1 remains open outside the regimes covered by their proof.

Remaining open problem. Prove this conjecture in general, or identify kernels/scalings for which the spectral trace criterion is not sharp.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The dense smooth-kernel threshold and several scaling extensions are proved. The open part is the proposed spectral criterion as a general law for smooth-kernel latent-geometry detection. Mao-Wu-Xu spectral conjecture

The hard-kernel RGG testing literature established that latent geometry can remain hidden in very high dimension and that signed subgraph statistics, especially signed triangles, are key in dense regimes. Sparse hard kernels required different techniques, with belief-propagation and posterior conditioning giving near-tight thresholds. Mao, Wu, and Xu move the problem to smooth kernels and show that smoothness changes the dominant statistics and lowers the dense critical dimension to n3/4n^{3/4}n3/4. Their spectral conjecture attempts to unify kernel shape, dimension, and signal-to-noise strength through the standardized operator trace. The gap is to prove both directions of the testing threshold for a broad kernel class, including contiguity below the threshold and an explicit or existential test above it.
