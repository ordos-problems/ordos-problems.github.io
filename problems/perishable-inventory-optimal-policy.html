Short Statement

Simple policies already have approximation and large-penalty optimality guarantees. The remaining target is sharper nonasymptotic control or exact structure at finite parameters, not the mere existence of asymptotic results.

Open Problem

For the specified FIFO lost-sales model at fixed finite parameters, establish a useful exact policy structure or sharper explicit performance bounds for a tractable policy. Exclude already settled high-penalty demand regimes and specify the benchmark and computational guarantees.

Background & Context

Age composition matters because two inventories with the same total stock can have very different future expiry losses. The oldest-first rule fixes issuance, leaving the order decision as the control. This creates a genuine multidimensional dynamic program even with one item and zero lead time. The policy literature now offers more than heuristics: approximation algorithms and high-penalty asymptotic theorems explain when relatively simple policies work. A useful open question must therefore isolate what these theorems leave unresolved. Here that is exact structure and finite-parameter performance outside the proved regimes. The positive-lead-time extension has additional pipeline information and a separate recent projected-inventory-level literature; its results should not be confused with the zero-lead-time baseline.

What Is Known

Classical work by Nahmias and Pierskalla and Nahmias's survey develops age-profile dynamic programming and short-lifetime special cases. Zhang, Ayer, and White's approximation work gives factor-two guarantees under its FIFO-optimality and finite-horizon assumptions; such a finite-horizon bound should not be silently treated as an infinite-horizon average-cost theorem. Bu, Gong, and Chao (Operations Research 72(5):1765-1774, 2024), Section 4 and Theorem 3, establish large-penalty asymptotics for zero-lead-time perishable inventory, including the iid lost-sales specialization above. For unbounded demand with E[D−x∣D>x]=o(x)\mathbb E[D-x\mid D>x]=o(x)E[D−x∣D>x]=o(x), the best base-stock cost is asymptotically optimal; Theorem 3(b) also covers continuously distributed regularly varying tails with exponent greater than one. Proposition 5 gives stronger bounded-demand conclusions, including exact optimality at sufficiently large penalty for bounded integer-valued demand. Thus even some finite-parameter cases are settled. Bu, Gong, and Yin's Management Science article, published online August 26, 2026, studies the different positive-lead-time model. Its publisher abstract reports large-penalty PIL optimality for specified unbounded-demand classes and special bounded-demand results. The full theorem assumptions were not accessible in this pass, so no universal claim for arbitrary demand, lead time, or issuance is made here. Remaining open problem.
