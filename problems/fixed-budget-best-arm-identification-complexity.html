Short Statement

Fixed-budget best-arm identification has no universally optimal instance-wise error exponent in general. The remaining issue is a sharp achievable tradeoff or a meaningful restricted algorithm class, not a universal analogue of Track-and-Stop.

Open Problem

The unrestricted uniformly optimal exponent is ruled out in general. The residual research direction is to identify a nontrivial restricted algorithm class with a sharp complexity, or a sharp benchmark-relative tradeoff; specify the class and reward family explicitly.

Background & Context

A fixed experimental budget is natural in simulation optimization and ranking and selection: the experiment ends after a predetermined number of samples, rather than when a confidence threshold is reached. The asymptotic question concerns rare erroneous recommendations. This is different from fixed-confidence sample complexity and from Bayesian posterior concentration. A sampling rule whose proportions converge almost surely to an oracle allocation need not inherit that allocation's frequentist error exponent: atypical trajectories can dominate errors. Qin's COLT 2022 note separated the existence of a common instance-wise optimum from the weaker question of improving on uniform allocation. The two questions now have substantial answers. This entry retains the historical question and its limitations, rather than continuing to advertise an unrestricted optimal algorithm as an unsolved target.

The historical question asked for an instance-dependent complexity attained simultaneously by one algorithm across all instances. That cannot hold in general: Degenne's COLT 2023 impossibility theorem already covers two Bernoulli arms for algorithm classes containing all fixed-proportion rules. Thus the previous formulation asking unconditionally for a uniformly optimal exponent was stale. A distinct residual problem, identified in Degenne's conclusion, is whether a meaningful restricted class admits such a complexity. The class must be specified before optimization; restricting to a singleton makes the question vacuous. Alternatively one can fix a benchmark and seek a minimax ratio across instances, rather than a pointwise optimum. These alternatives must not be reported as the original unrestricted conjecture. The separate question of dominating uniform sampling also depends on the reward family: the two-arm Bernoulli case is settled negatively by Wang, Ariu, and Proutiere, while Imbens, Qin, and Wager give a positive Gaussian equal-variance result for K≥3K\ge3K≥3.

What Is Known

Degenne (COLT 2023, Theorem 10) rules out a common attainable complexity for two-arm Bernoulli identification in classes containing all static-proportion algorithms; the paper also establishes an obstruction for sufficiently many Gaussian arms. Its conclusion asks about meaningful restricted algorithm classes. Wang, Ariu, and Proutiere (ICML 2024) show that uniform allocation is undominated in error-exponent performance for two Bernoulli arms. Imbens, Qin, and Wager (2025 preprint, Assumption 1 and Theorem 5) construct batched elimination rules improving the exponent over uniform allocation for K≥3K\ge3K≥3 Gaussian arms with common variance. For a fixed unique-best instance, expected simple regret and error probability differ by fixed positive gap factors, so they have the same exponential rate. Goldberger's July 2026 preprint, Theorems 3.2 and 4.3, reports nonexistence of a complexity for every K≥3K\ge3K≥3 in regular one-parameter natural exponential families. This is a preprint result, not a proof independently certified by this repository. The unresolved work is on restricted classes or alternative tradeoffs, not the original unrestricted existence claim.

Remaining open problem. The unrestricted uniformly optimal exponent is ruled out in general. The residual research direction is to identify a nontrivial restricted algorithm class with a sharp complexity, or a sharp benchmark-relative tradeoff; specify the class and reward family explicitly.
