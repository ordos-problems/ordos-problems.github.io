Short Statement

Find a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for Exact Matching, the problem of deciding whether a graph has a perfect matching with exactly kkk red edges.

Open Problem

Find a verified deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for Exact Matching on arbitrary graphs. A 2026 bipartite claim is recorded below with a concrete issue in a subsidiary statement; it does not settle the general-graph problem.

Background & Context

Exact Matching is a deceptively small extension of ordinary perfect matching. Ordinary matching is one of the central polynomial-time problems in combinatorial optimization, but adding the exact cardinality constraint on one color class moves the known general algorithmic status into randomized polynomial time. The problem also appears as a hardness core inside robust and recoverable assignment models, so it is not just a complexity-theory curiosity. The randomized algorithm of Mulmuley, Vazirani, and Vazirani is based on algebraic isolation: assign random weights so that a relevant matching is isolated with good probability, then use determinant or Pfaffian computations. Derandomizing this step is a long-standing open problem connected to the broader project of derandomizing isolation lemmas. Recent deterministic fixed-parameter algorithms show that structure helps, but the unrestricted deterministic polynomial-time question remains open.

A satisfactory positive solution may derandomize the isolation step, avoid isolation through a different algebraic certificate, or exploit the matching polytope and exact-cardinality constraint in a new way.

What Is Known

Mulmuley, Vazirani, and Vazirani gave a randomized polynomial-time algorithm via the isolation lemma and algebraic matching computations. The same isolation paradigm underlies many randomized matching algorithms and complexity-theoretic unique-witness reductions. Deterministic algorithms are known for ordinary perfect matching and for several structurally restricted Exact Matching instances. El Maalouly, Steiner, and Wulf gave deterministic FPT algorithms for bipartite graphs parameterized by bipartite independence number. Murakami and Yamaguchi extended deterministic FPT algorithms to more general graphs using odd-cycle-transversal and independence parameters. Fischer, Hartmann, Lendl, and Woeginger show that robust/recoverable assignment variants inherit the unresolved status of Exact Matching. None of these results yields a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for unrestricted Exact Matching. 2026 claim requiring scrutiny. Exact Matching is close enough to ordinary matching that one expects combinatorial structure, but the exact red-edge constraint blocks direct use of standard augmenting-path algorithms. The randomized MVV approach succeeds by isolating a witness matching and detecting it algebraically; derandomizing this would require deterministic isolation for the relevant family of perfect matchings or a different certificate. Parameterized results show that deterministic algorithms can exploit low independence number or small odd-cycle-transversal structure, while robust assignment reductions show the problem's influence in operations-research models. The unresolved gap is the general graph case with no structural parameter. A 2026 bipartite manuscript is a claim to investigate, not an established improvement in this entry: Theorem 2.7(iii) has the elementary affine-slice counterexample described above, and the Lean development assumes eight structural inputs. Neither the defect in that subsidiary identity nor the incomplete formalization alone proves the main theorem false.
