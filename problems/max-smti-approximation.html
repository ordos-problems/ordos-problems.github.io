Background & Context

The stable marriage problem of Gale and Shapley (1962) is the founding model of matching under preferences: two sides rank each other, and a matching is stable if no pair would rather be together than with their assigned partners. In real deployments — hospital-resident matching (e.g., the Scottish Foundation Allocation Scheme), school choice, and labor markets — agents routinely submit preference lists that are both incomplete (they rank only acceptable partners) and contain ties (indifference among several partners). This combination, SMTI, changes the problem fundamentally. Under the standard notion of weak stability, stable matchings still always exist and one can be found by breaking ties arbitrarily and running Gale-Shapley, but different weakly stable matchings can have different sizes — up to a factor of two apart. Since leaving participants unmatched is costly in applications (an unmatched resident, an unfilled position), the natural objective is MAX-SMTI: find a maximum-cardinality weakly stable matching. Iwama, Manlove, Miyazaki, and Morita (1999) proved this NP-hard, and it was soon shown APX-hard even under severe restrictions (e.g., ties of length two on one side only). This launched a long approximation race — from the trivial factor 2 through 15/8, 5/3, and 8/5 — culminating in McDermid's 3/2-approximation at ICALP 2009, later simplified and made linear-time by Paluch and by Király, and recently extended by Csáji, Király, and Yokoi to a wide range of generalizations (matroid constraints, free edges, critical agents) still at ratio exactly 3/2. Meanwhile the hardness frontier advanced from 21/19 to Yanagisawa's 33/29 (assuming P ≠ NP) and 4/3 under the Unique Games Conjecture, and — importantly — Dudycz, Lewandowski, and Marcinkowski (IPCO 2019) showed that under the Small Set Expansion Hypothesis or a strong form of UGC, no (3/2−ε)(3/2-\varepsilon)(3/2−ε)-approximation exists at all. Determining the true approximation threshold, and in particular whether 3/2 can be beaten under only P ≠ NP or standard UGC, is one of the best-known open problems in the algorithmics of matching under preferences (it is highlighted in Manlove's monograph and recurs in the open-problem lists of the field).

Latest progress (Partial progress). Two lines of attack dominate. Algorithmically, all 3/23/23/2-approximations descend from Gale-Shapley augmented with mechanisms to reduce the number of length-3 augmenting paths between the output and an optimal matching: McDermid (ICALP 2009) first achieved 3/23/23/2, Paluch and Király gave linear-time versions, and Csáji, Király, and Yokoi (Math. Oper. Res. 2025; arXiv:2304.02558) distilled the approach into an edge-duplication technique that extends the ratio to matroid constraints, free edges, and critical agents — but every known charging argument saturates at 3/23/23/2 because a stable matching can miss one vertex of each of OPT\mathrm{OPT}OPT's length-3 alternating paths. On the hardness side, Halldórsson, Iwama, Miyazaki, and Yanagisawa (ACM Trans. Algorithms 2007) and Yanagisawa's 2007 thesis established 33/2933/2933/29 NP-hardness and 4/34/34/3 UGC-hardness, and Dudycz, Lewandowski, and Marcinkowski (IPCO 2019) leveraged the tight connection to minimum maximal matching to show that no (3/2−ε)(3/2-\varepsilon)(3/2−ε)-approximation exists under the Small Set Expansion Hypothesis or strong UGC — strong evidence that 3/23/23/2 is the true threshold. The remaining gap is thus really a question about complexity assumptions: beating 3/23/23/2 would refute SSEH, while confirming 3/23/23/2-hardness under vanilla UGC or P ≠\ne= NP needs new gadget constructions beyond the current 4/34/34/3 and 33/2933/2933/29 reductions. Restricted variants show the algorithmic frontier is not rigid everywhere: one-sided ties admit 1+1/e1+1/e1+1/e (Lam-Plaxton, SODA 2019) against a (1+1/e)(1+1/e)(1+1/e)-matching UGC lower bound conjectured tight, and ties of size ≤L\le L≤L admit (3L−2)/(2L−1)(3L-2)/(2L-1)(3L−2)/(2L−1) (Koenemann-Pashkovich-Tofigzade, SAGT 2020). New ideas are most needed on the unconditional hardness side.
