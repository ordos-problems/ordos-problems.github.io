Background & Context

Bin packing and cutting stock are core problems in approximation algorithms, integer programming, and operations research. The configuration LP is unusually strong: although it has exponentially many columns, it captures the natural pattern structure of bins and underlies the Karmarkar-Karp approximation scheme. Most approximation questions ask for multiplicative ratios, but this problem asks for an additive constant, which is much sharper and directly relevant in large cutting-stock instances where a few extra bins matter. The problem is also connected to discrepancy theory: rounding a fractional collection of configurations to integer bins is a vector-balancing problem with additional packing structure. Hoberg and Rothvoss's logarithmic bound shows that discrepancy methods go far, but their own framing indicates that further progress likely needs more algebraic or structural use of bin-packing configurations.
