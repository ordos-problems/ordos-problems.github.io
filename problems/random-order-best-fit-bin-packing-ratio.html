Short Statement

In online bin packing under uniformly random arrival order, determine the exact asymptotic performance ratio of the classical Best-Fit algorithm.

Open Problem

Determine this constant ρ\rhoρ.

Background & Context

Bin packing is one of the canonical models of resource allocation: items of different sizes must be assigned to the minimum number of unit-capacity bins. The online version models packing, memory allocation, scheduling, and admission decisions when future jobs are unknown. Worst-case online bin packing is deliberately pessimistic; adversarial order lets an input designer reveal items in a sequence that is much worse than a typical shuffled instance. Kenyon's random-order model keeps the multiset adversarial but randomizes the arrival order, creating a clean beyond-worst-case benchmark. Best-Fit is especially important because it is simple, deterministic, and used in practice. The random-order analysis asks whether the empirical robustness of Best-Fit has a theorem-level explanation. The problem is also technically attractive: it mixes online irrevocability, random permutations, and packing structure. The main difficulty is that Best-Fit creates state-dependent residual capacities; early random fluctuations determine which gaps remain for later items, and these dependencies are hard to summarize by a small set of statistics.

What Is Known

Kenyon's SODA 1996 work established an upper bound of 1.51.51.5 and a lower bound of 1.081.081.08 for the random-order ratio of Best-Fit, and conjectured a value around 1.151.151.15. The literature has progressed by separating item-size regimes. Kenyon gave the baseline random-order framework and the original 1.51.51.5 upper bound. Later work improved special intervals such as items larger than 1/31/31/3 or in (1/4,1/2](1/4,1/2](1/4,1/2], where the packing structure is much more constrained. Hebbar, Khan, and Sreenivas showed that the full problem is not stuck at 3/23/23/2, but their improvement is deliberately small and technical.
