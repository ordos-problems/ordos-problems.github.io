Short Statement

Characterize the exact stability region of adaptive limited-service polling systems, including whether full primitive distributions are unavoidable.

Open Problem

Find the exact stability criterion for adaptive polling systems with general limited-service disciplines, and determine the minimal distributional information needed to state it.

Background & Context

Stability of queueing networks is often summarized by a traffic-intensity condition, but adaptive polling systems can be more subtle. In the model of Chernova, Foss, and Kim, a single server cycles among queues and may skip a queue in the next cycle if it found that queue empty in the current cycle. Under limited service, each visit serves only a bounded number of customers. The natural fluid-limit approach gives sufficient stability and instability conditions in some cases, but the conditions do not always meet. The authors conjectured that this is not merely a proof artifact: for some adaptive limited-service polling systems, stability may depend on the full primitive distributions, not on a finite collection of moments. A follow-up example supports this view by showing dependence on an exponential moment of the service-time distribution. The open problem is to turn these partial criteria and examples into an exact stability characterization.

Statement & Model

Consider a cyclic polling system with a single server and finitely many queues. Arrivals, service times, and switch-over times are generated by specified renewal or iid primitive sequences. The server normally visits queues cyclically, but follows an adaptive rule: if it finds a queue empty during a cycle, it may skip that queue in the next cycle. Under a limited-service discipline, at most a fixed number of customers are served per visit, so work can remain behind even after service. The stochastic process of interest is the queue-length vector observed in continuous time or at polling epochs. The open problem is to determine necessary and sufficient conditions for positive recurrence for the general adaptive limited-service class. In particular, decide whether the stability region can be expressed using finitely many moments of the primitives or whether transforms, exponential moments, or the entire service/interarrival distributions are unavoidable.

What Is Known

Chernova, Foss, and Kim derived stability and instability conditions for several adaptive polling models, but for general limited-service disciplines the sufficient conditions leave a gap. They conjectured that the exact condition may depend on entire primitive distributions. In a follow-up paper they constructed a polling system whose stability region depends on an exponential moment of a service-time distribution, showing that finite moment summaries can be insufficient in related adaptive polling models. Broader polling-system work gives exact transforms, heavy-traffic approximations, and open problems for gated, exhaustive, and mixed disciplines, but those classical tools do not directly close the adaptive limited-service stability gap. The unresolved issue is a full positive-recurrence/transience classification for the adaptive rule.

Remaining open problem. Find the exact stability criterion for adaptive polling systems with general limited-service disciplines, and determine the minimal distributional information needed to state it.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Evidence now shows that whole-distribution dependence can occur in adaptive polling. The exact stability region for the original broader limited-service class remains open. The key difficulty is that adaptive skipping changes future server availability based on past emptiness events, and those events can encode more than average arrival and service rates. Standard fluid limits may wash out details that still affect whether the system empties often enough to skip queues. Chernova, Foss, and Kim's later example strengthens the original conjectural picture: stability can depend on an exponential moment, not just finitely many power moments. General polling-system theory provides transform and heavy-traffic methods for classical service disciplines, and Boon-Boxma-Winands document nearby open polling problems. The open task is to decide whether a general exact condition can be written in terms of transforms/regeneration quantities, or whether only model-by-model criteria are possible.
