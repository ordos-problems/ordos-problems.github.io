Background & Context

Historical context before the 2026 full-resolution claims: The Komlos conjecture is a central vector-balancing problem. Given vectors of Euclidean norm at most one, choose signs so that every coordinate of the signed sum is bounded by a universal constant. It generalizes Beck-Fiala because the incidence columns of a degree-ttt set system can be scaled to unit length, and a constant Komlos bound would imply O(t)O(\sqrt t)O(t​) Beck-Fiala discrepancy. The conjecture is also a benchmark for algorithmic discrepancy, convex geometry, and rounding. Banaszczyk's theorem gives the best classical asymptotic bound O(log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n})O(logn​), and recent work of Bansal and Jiang improves this to roughly O~(log⁡1/4n)\widetilde O(\log^{1/4} n)O(log1/4n), but the desired dimension-free constant was not settled by those earlier results. Status at 2026-11-09: Guo, Fang, and Lu's September 2026 preprint claims the full Komlos conjecture (Theorem 1.1); Karingula and Lovett give a subsequent elementary-proof preprint.

Equivalently, let A∈Rm×nA\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}A∈Rm×n be a real matrix whose columns have ℓ2\ell_2ℓ2​ norm at most 111. A signing x∈{−1,+1}nx\in\{-1,+1\}^nx∈{−1,+1}n has discrepancy ∥Ax∥∞\|Ax\|_\infty∥Ax∥∞​, the largest absolute coordinate of the signed vector sum. The Komlos conjecture asks whether there is a universal constant KKK such that every such matrix admits a signing with ∥Ax∥∞≤K\|Ax\|_\infty\le K∥Ax∥∞​≤K, independent of the number of rows mmm and columns nnn. The conjecture implies Beck-Fiala after scaling each degree-ttt incidence column by 1/t1/\sqrt t1/t​: a constant Komlos bound would give O(t)O(\sqrt t)O(t​) discrepancy for degree-ttt set systems. The historically open problem is the full dimension-free constant bound for arbitrary real unit-column matrices, with no distributional, smoothed-input, bounded-rank, or sparsity assumption. An algorithmic solution would additionally find the signing in polynomial time. What Is Known

Historical context before the 2026 full-resolution claims: Banaszczyk proved that unit-column matrices admit discrepancy O(log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n})O(logn​), and Bansal, Dadush, and Garg gave an efficient algorithm matching that bound. The conjectured answer is O(1)O(1)O(1). Bansal, Jiang, Meka, Singla, and Sinha proved Komlos in a smoothed-analysis setting when the input vectors are perturbed and sufficiently numerous relative to the dimension. Bansal and Jiang recently improved the pre-September-2026 worst-case general dependence to O~(log⁡1/4n)\widetilde O(\log^{1/4}n)O(log1/4n) using affine spectral-independence constraints in an SDP-guided random walk. They also obtain improved Beck-Fiala consequences. Lower bounds show that the optimal universal constant, if the conjecture holds, cannot be too small, but no superconstant lower bound is known. The pre-September-2026 gap was between constant and about a fourth-root logarithm. Status at 2026-11-09: Guo, Fang, and Lu's September 2026 preprint claims the full Komlos conjecture (Theorem 1.1); Karingula and Lovett give a subsequent elementary-proof preprint. Algorithmic follow-up: Guo, Fang, and Lu, arXiv:2609.23540v2, Theorem 1.1, report a deterministic polynomial-time real-RAM algorithm with discrepancy at most 827282728272.
