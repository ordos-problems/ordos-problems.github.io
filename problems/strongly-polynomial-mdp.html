Short Statement

Is there a strongly polynomial algorithm for general finite discounted MDPs when the discount factor is part of the input?

Open Problem

Give a strongly polynomial algorithm for general discounted MDPs with input discount factor, or prove that policy iteration/simplex-type algorithms cannot be strongly polynomial in a precise model. The open issue is the regime γ→1\gamma \to 1γ→1 for stochastic MDPs, not fixed-discount MDPs.

Background & Context

Finite Markov decision processes are the dynamic-programming analogue of linear programming: a finite set of states, a finite set of actions, transition probabilities, and rewards define an optimal control problem. Discounted MDPs can be solved by value iteration, policy iteration, or LP formulations, and these methods are fundamental in OR, reinforcement learning, and stochastic control. Complexity becomes delicate when the discount factor γ\gammaγ approaches one. A bound polynomial in 1/(1−γ)1/(1-\gamma)1/(1−γ) is useful for fixed discount, but it is not strongly polynomial when γ\gammaγ is part of the input. Ye proved a strong polynomiality result for fixed γ\gammaγ, and Post and Ye later obtained strongly polynomial simplex bounds for deterministic MDPs independent of the discount factor. The general stochastic case remains unresolved and is closely connected to whether special LP structure can be exploited more deeply than in arbitrary linear programming.

What Is Known

Ye (2011) proved polynomial iteration bounds for Howard policy iteration and the simplex method for discounted MDPs with a fixed discount factor; more precisely, the bounds scale polynomially in n,mn,mn,m and in quantities such as 1/(1−γ)1/(1-\gamma)1/(1−γ), so they are strongly polynomial only when the discount factor is treated as a fixed constant. Hansen, Miltersen, and Zwick improved and extended fixed-discount strategy-iteration analysis to two-player turn-based stochastic games, again with explicit dependence on 1/(1−γ)1/(1-\gamma)1/(1−γ). Post and Ye proved a genuinely strongly polynomial simplex bound for deterministic MDPs independent of the discount factor, using layered progress/milestone arguments; their result covers deterministic transitions, not general stochastic transition matrices. General finite discounted MDPs with stochastic transitions and input discount factor remain open. The average-reward/undiscounted limiting regime is closely related because hard behavior may appear as γ\gammaγ approaches one.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The deterministic case is strongly polynomial, and fixed-discount stochastic MDPs have polynomial iteration bounds. The unresolved gap is stochastic transitions with discount factor encoded in the input or average-reward limiting behavior. The simplex method is strongly polynomial for deterministic Markov decision processes

Known results show a sharp divide between fixed-discount or deterministic structure and the fully stochastic input-discount case. Ye's analysis gives polynomial iteration bounds for Howard policy iteration and simplex when the discount factor is fixed, but the dependence on 1/(1−γ)1/(1-\gamma)1/(1−γ) is exactly what prevents a strongly polynomial theorem for input discounts near one. Post and Ye remove the discount dependence for deterministic MDPs by exploiting the structure of deterministic transitions: the induced policy graph has cycles and paths whose primal/dual values move through a polynomial number of layers or milestones. In stochastic MDPs, transition matrices mix states, values can change gradually, and the deterministic graph-layer argument no longer applies. A solution could come from a new progress measure for policy iteration, a different combinatorial algorithm for the Bellman inequalities, or a reduction to a special LP class with known strongly polynomial algorithms. A negative result for a specific policy-iteration rule would be useful but would not rule out all strongly polynomial algorithms.
