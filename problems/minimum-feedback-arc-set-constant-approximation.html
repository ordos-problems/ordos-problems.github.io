Short Statement

Determine whether minimum feedback arc set in general directed graphs has a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation under standard assumptions.

Open Problem

Does general directed minimum feedback arc set admit a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation, or is every polynomial-time approximation necessarily superconstant under a standard complexity assumption?

Background & Context

Minimum feedback arc set is a clean ordering problem: find the order of vertices that minimizes the total weight of arcs pointing backward. In tournaments this is the Kemeny/rank-aggregation problem and admits much stronger approximation schemes, but general directed graphs are far less understood. The model is not an application-specific modeling question; it is a fixed graph optimization problem with a direct LP/ordering formulation. The contrast with related problems is striking. Undirected feedback vertex set has constant approximations, maximum acyclic subgraph has a simple constant approximation, and tournament feedback arc set has PTAS-type results. But the minimization version for arbitrary directed graphs remains stuck at a polylogarithmic approximation versus only constant-factor hardness under standard assumptions.

Statement & Model

Given a directed graph G=(V,A)G=(V,A)G=(V,A) with nonnegative arc weights waw_awa​, a feedback arc set is a set F⊆AF\subseteq AF⊆A whose removal makes GGG acyclic. Equivalently, one can seek a linear ordering of the vertices and pay for arcs directed backward in the ordering; the minimum feedback arc set value is the minimum backward-arc weight over all orders. The problem is NP-hard and is one of the classical cycle-breaking and ranking problems, with applications in deadlock resolution, circuit analysis, graph drawing, and rank aggregation. The best general approximation algorithms for directed feedback arc set are polylogarithmic rather than constant, while strong hardness is known only under assumptions such as the Unique Games Conjecture. The open problem is to determine whether a polynomial-time O(1)O(1)O(1)-approximation exists for weighted or unweighted minimum feedback arc set in general directed graphs under ordinary assumptions such as P≠NPP\ne NPP=NP, or whether a superconstant lower bound can be proved.

What Is Known

Karp's original NP-completeness framework includes feedback arc/feedback vertex style cycle-breaking problems, and later work established APX-hardness for directed feedback arc set. Even, Naor, Schieber, and Sudan gave influential polylogarithmic approximation algorithms for minimum feedback sets and directed multicuts; these remain part of the standard approximation baseline. For dense or tournament inputs the situation is much better: feedback arc set in tournaments admits polynomial-time approximation schemes, reflecting the additional ordering structure of complete orientations. Hardness is delicate. Under the Unique Games Conjecture, directed feedback arc set and directed feedback vertex set are hard to approximate within any constant factor, but that conditional result does not give an unconditional superconstant lower bound. Under P≠NPP\ne NPP=NP, the known inapproximability is only constant. Thus the central gap is qualitative: either find a constant approximation for arbitrary directed graphs, or prove superconstant hardness from more standard assumptions.

The approximation landscape is asymmetric. Maximizing the number of forward arcs is easy to approximate by taking the better of an order and its reverse, but minimizing the backward arcs is sensitive to the optimum being small. Tournament structure removes sparse pathologies and leads to PTAS results, which is why rank aggregation is algorithmically different from general directed cycle breaking. The Even-Naor-Schieber-Sudan framework connects feedback sets with directed multicut and gives polylogarithmic guarantees, but it does not compress to a constant. Unique-Games hardness suggests a constant approximation may be impossible, yet the repository standard distinguishes UGC evidence from standard-assumption resolution. A breakthrough would either find a new decomposition of directed cycles into orderable pieces or convert conditional hardness intuition into an unconditional superconstant lower bound.
