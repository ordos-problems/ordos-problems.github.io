Short Statement

Uniform-prior adaptive identification has strong greedy and subexponential-time guarantees, but the reported 2026 polynomial-time constant approximation was withdrawn.

Open Problem

Find a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation for Uniform Decision Tree, or establish an appropriate stronger hardness result. The withdrawn 2026 constant-factor claim and fixed-order Pandora guarantees do not settle this question.

Background & Context

Adaptive identification is useful in diagnosis, active learning, and sequential testing. Uniform priors change the approximation landscape: hardness for an arbitrary prior cannot simply be transferred to equally likely hypotheses. A tree can be optimized by exhaustive recursion on subsets, but this is exponential. The central issue is whether a polynomial-time method can exploit the uniform prior as effectively as the known subexponential algorithms.

Statement & Model

Let HHH be a set of nnn hypotheses, with a uniform prior, and let TTT be an explicitly given family of tests. Each unit-cost test is a map τ:H→[K]\tau:H\to[K]τ:H→[K] specifying the observed outcome for every hypothesis. Assume the tests separate every pair of hypotheses. A decision tree chooses a test at each internal node, branches on its outcome, and identifies the hidden hypothesis at a leaf. Its cost is C(D)=n−1∑h∈Hdepth⁡D(h)C(D)=n^{-1}\sum_{h\in H}\operatorname{depth}_D(h)C(D)=n−1∑h∈H​depthD​(h), and OPT\mathrm{OPT}OPT is the minimum cost over valid trees. Does a polynomial-time algorithm always construct a tree with C(D)≤c OPTC(D)\le c\,\mathrm{OPT}C(D)≤cOPT for a universal constant ccc? State whether the result covers arbitrary outcome arity KKK or only fixed KKK; even binary tests form a meaningful restricted target. Time must be polynomial in the explicit test table, not subexponential in nnn. Nonuniform hypothesis priors and nonuniform test costs are different models. The constant-factor claim in arXiv:2604.12036v1 was withdrawn in v2; the current v4 paper does not resolve this target.

What Is Known

Li, Liang, and Mussmann (arXiv:1906.11385v2), Theorems 3.1-3.2, bound greedy's ratio by O(log⁡n/log⁡COPT)O(\log n/\log C_{\mathrm{OPT}})O(logn/logCOPT​) and obtain constant approximations in subexponential time. For fixed outcome arity, this gives a polynomial-time O(log⁡n/log⁡log⁡n)O(\log n/\log\log n)O(logn/loglogn) approximation. Their cited hardness threshold is 4−ε4-\varepsilon4−ε; their ETH implication limits superconstant NP-hardness results. Szyfelbein's arXiv:2604.12036v1 claimed a constant ratio, but v2 (April 28, 2026) withdraws it because of a fundamental proof error and a counterexample to the algorithm. Version 4, 'Simpler Logarithmic Approximation Algorithms for the Optimal Decision Tree and Adaptive Set Cover' (October 19, 2026), instead gives logarithmic simultaneous average- and worst-case guarantees. The earlier claim is not current evidence of resolution. Chawla, Gergatsouli, McMahan, and Tzamos connect UDT to fully adaptive correlated Pandora policies. Bansal, Huang, and Zhu's 2025 ratio-4 result instead benchmarks partially adaptive policies: a fixed box order with adaptive stopping (Section 2.2). It cannot be transferred as a resolution of fully adaptive UDT. The polynomial-time constant-factor question therefore remains open in the reviewed sources.

Remaining open problem. Find a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation for Uniform Decision Tree, or establish an appropriate stronger hardness result. The withdrawn 2026 constant-factor claim and fixed-order Pandora guarantees do not settle this question.
