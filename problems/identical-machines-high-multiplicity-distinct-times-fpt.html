Background & Context

High-multiplicity encoding is natural in operations settings with many identical jobs of a few types: production lots, cloud tasks, appointment templates, and repeated batch work. It changes the algorithmic question because the explicit number of jobs may be exponentially larger than the input length. For ordinary explicit scheduling, a small number of processing times often enables dynamic programming. Under compact multiplicities, the same intuition can fail because counts are large binary numbers and the algorithm must reason about integer decompositions of machine loads. Mnich and van Bevern include this problem in their parameterized scheduling list as a clean open case at the interface of scheduling, integer programming, and fixed-parameter algorithms. The 2025 high-multiplicity scheduling work sharpens the integer-cone toolkit but did not settle the fully binary case. The September 2026 preprint now claims to do so.
