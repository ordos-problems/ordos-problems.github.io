Short Statement

The Gupta-Newman-Rabinovich-Sinclair conjecture asks whether every fixed-minor-free graph family has constant multicommodity flow-cut gap.

Open Problem

Background & Context

Multicommodity flow is a central relaxation in network design, routing, graph partitioning, and approximation algorithms. The cut condition is the most basic necessary condition for routing demands, but it is not sufficient in general graphs without capacity augmentation. The worst-case gap between cuts and flows is Θ(log⁡n)\Theta(\log n)Θ(logn) in general graphs and underlies the logarithmic approximability of Sparsest Cut. Minor-free graph families are much more structured and include planar graphs, bounded-genus graphs, and many networks that arise in geometric or infrastructure settings. The GNRS conjecture says that this structure should collapse the logarithmic gap to a constant. A proof would have algorithmic consequences for routing and cut problems on minor-free networks; a counterexample would show that even topologically simple networks can hide arbitrarily bad multicommodity obstructions.

The open problem is to prove this constant bound or construct a fixed-minor-free family with unbounded flow-cut gap.

What Is Known

General graphs have logarithmic flow-cut gaps, characterized through metric embeddings. The GNRS conjecture predicts constant gaps for every fixed-minor-free family. Several special cases and related bounds are known. Chekuri, Shepherd, and Weibel prove constant integer or fractional flow-cut gap results for important subclasses such as series-parallel and kkk-outerplanar graphs, and they formulate a stronger integer-flow conjecture related to the fractional one. Salmasi, Sidiropoulos, and Sridhar describe the undirected GNRS conjecture as the standard benchmark and obtain first constant directed flow-cut gap results for directed series-parallel graphs, directed bounded-pathwidth graphs, directed trees, and directed cycles. However, the core undirected conjecture remains open even at the level of proving a constant bound for broad fixed-minor-free classes beyond the known special cases.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Constant bounds are known for special graph classes, but the fixed-minor-free conjecture remains open.

The GNRS conjecture connects three languages: routing, cuts, and metric embeddings. The routing language asks how much one must scale capacities after all cut inequalities hold. The cut language views this as the integrality/duality gap behind sparsest-cut relaxations. The metric language asks whether shortest-path metrics of minor-free graphs embed into ℓ1\ell_1ℓ1​ with distortion depending only on the excluded minor. General graphs need logarithmic distortion, so the conjecture is a strong structural claim about topology. Known positive results for trees, cycles, series-parallel graphs, and bounded outerplanarity support the conjecture, but the techniques do not yet handle arbitrary planar or fixed-minor-free interactions. A proof may require a new decomposition of minor-free metrics; a counterexample would likely expose a multiscale demand pattern not captured by existing separator or embedding methods.
