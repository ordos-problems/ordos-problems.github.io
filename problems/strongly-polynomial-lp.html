Short Statement

Does every linear program admit a strongly polynomial algorithm, with operation count bounded polynomially in only the number of variables and constraints?

Open Problem

Construct a strongly polynomial algorithm for general rational linear programming, or prove that no such algorithm exists in a standard algebraic/bit model. The open part is not polynomial-time solvability of LP, but the removal of all dependence on numerical magnitudes, encoding length, or condition measures from the iteration count.

Background & Context

Linear programming is one of the core algorithmic models of Operations Research: production planning, flows, matching, auctions, statistical estimation, and many relaxations for hard discrete problems are all expressed as LPs. The ellipsoid method and interior-point methods show that LP is solvable in time polynomial in the input bit length, but this is a weakly polynomial guarantee. A strongly polynomial algorithm would use a number of arithmetic operations polynomial only in the numbers of variables and constraints, and would keep intermediate numbers under polynomial bit control. This distinction matters because LP is also the canonical test case for whether continuous optimization admits a genuinely combinatorial complexity theory. Many special LP families have strongly polynomial algorithms, and Tardos's framework explains why bounded-coefficient combinatorial LPs behave better than arbitrary rational LPs. Smale listed the general problem as one of his mathematical problems for the next century. Despite decades of progress in scaling, conditioning, and interior-point methods, no algorithm is known whose worst-case complexity removes all dependence on coefficient magnitudes or condition numbers.

Statement & Model

Given a rational linear program, for example min⁡{c⊤x:Ax≥b}\min\{c^\top x: Ax \ge b\}min{c⊤x:Ax≥b} with A∈Qm×nA \in \mathbb{Q}^{m \times n}A∈Qm×n, b∈Qmb \in \mathbb{Q}^mb∈Qm, and c∈Qnc \in \mathbb{Q}^nc∈Qn, decide feasibility and, when bounded, return an optimal solution. Is there an algorithm whose number of arithmetic operations is bounded by poly⁡(m,n)\operatorname{poly}(m,n)poly(m,n) and whose intermediate numbers have bit length polynomial in the input bit length? Equivalently, can general LP be solved in strongly polynomial time rather than merely weakly polynomial time?

What Is Known

Khachiyan's ellipsoid method and Karmarkar/interior-point methods give weakly polynomial algorithms. Tardos (1986) gave strongly polynomial algorithms for LPs whose constraint matrix has small integral entries, covering many combinatorial optimization problems. Fixed-dimensional LP and several structured network and matrix classes are also strongly polynomial. Modern work by Dadush, Huiberts, Natura, and Vegh gives scaling-invariant algorithms whose complexity depends on geometric condition measures of the constraint matrix, which is important progress but still not a general strongly polynomial bound. As of 2026, the unrestricted problem remains open.

Remaining open problem. Construct a strongly polynomial algorithm for general rational linear programming, or prove that no such algorithm exists in a standard algebraic/bit model. The open part is not polynomial-time solvability of LP, but the removal of all dependence on numerical magnitudes, encoding length, or condition measures from the iteration count.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Special structured LPs have strongly polynomial algorithms, and recent condition-based algorithms reduce the role of data scaling. The remaining open problem is the fully general LP with no condition-number or coefficient-size dependence in the arithmetic count. A scaling-invariant algorithm for linear programming whose running time depends only on the constraint matrix

The main positive line is to identify LP families whose arithmetic complexity is controlled by combinatorial structure rather than coefficient size. Tardos's bounded-coefficient framework remains the most influential strongly polynomial theorem for broad combinatorial LP classes. Interior-point and ellipsoid algorithms solve general LP in weakly polynomial time, but their iteration and bit-complexity analyses still depend on input encoding or conditioning. Dadush, Huiberts, Natura, and Vegh developed a scaling-invariant approach whose running time depends on the constraint matrix through geometric condition measures; this clarifies why some numerical instances are tractable, but it does not eliminate all instance-geometry dependence. The remaining gap is therefore very crisp: no known method gives a poly⁡(m,n)\operatorname{poly}(m,n)poly(m,n) arithmetic bound for arbitrary rational LP, and no lower-bound theory rules such an algorithm out.
