Short Statement

Prove the Eschenfeldt-Gamarnik conjecture for the stationary short-queue profile of JSQ(ddd) in the many-server heavy-traffic supermarket model.

Open Problem

Prove or refute the conjectured stationary fixed-point limit for the short-queue profile in the heavy-traffic supermarket model.

Background & Context

The supermarket model is a canonical queueing model for randomized load balancing. There are nnn parallel single-server queues, arrivals occur at total rate nλn\lambdanλ, each arrival samples ddd queues uniformly at random, and the job joins a shortest sampled queue. For fixed λ<1\lambda<1λ<1, the power-of-ddd rule produces a doubly exponential stationary queue-length tail and a maximum queue length of order log⁡log⁡n\log\log nloglogn. Heavy traffic is subtler: as λn↑1\lambda_n\uparrow 1λn​↑1, the typical queue length grows, and the behavior of queues shorter than the typical scale is not diffusive. Eschenfeldt and Gamarnik identify a deterministic infinite-dimensional dynamical system for the short-queue profile and compute its unique fixed point. They conjecture that this fixed point is the limiting stationary short-queue profile. The open problem is to prove the steady-state version, i.e. an interchange-of-limits theorem and stationary convergence result for this heavy-traffic mean-field system.

What Is Known

Classical supermarket-model work by Vvedenskaya, Dobrushin, and Karpelevich and by Mitzenmacher established the mean-field picture for fixed load λ<1\lambda<1λ<1, including the power-of-two phenomenon and doubly exponential queue tails. Luczak and McDiarmid proved rapid mixing and showed that, in equilibrium for fixed λ<1\lambda<1λ<1, the maximum queue length concentrates on at most two adjacent values around log⁡log⁡n/log⁡d\log\log n/\log dloglogn/logd. Eschenfeldt and Gamarnik analyze the regime λn↑1\lambda_n\uparrow1λn​↑1. They prove that most queues have length at least log⁡d(1−λn)−1−O(1)\log_d(1-\lambda_n)^{-1}-O(1)logd​(1−λn​)−1−O(1) with high probability and derive a deterministic infinite-dimensional description for the dynamics of shorter queues after rescaling. They compute the unique fixed point π1(di−1)/(d−1)\pi_1(d^i-1)/(d-1)π1​(di−1)/(d−1) and explicitly conjecture that it describes the steady-state short-queue profile. The remaining difficulty is the steady-state limit: proving tightness, controlling the stationary process, and interchanging the limit in nnn with the long-time limit.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Transient rescaled dynamics and the candidate fixed point are known. The stationary convergence/interchange-of-limits theorem remains open. Supermarket Queueing System in the Heavy Traffic Regime. Short Queue Dynamics On the maximum queue length in the supermarket model

This is a precise open problem about a classical load-balancing queue. The fixed-load supermarket model is well understood, but heavy traffic changes the scale and removes the usual diffusion intuition for short queues. Eschenfeldt and Gamarnik provide the limiting dynamical equations and the unique candidate fixed point, so the remaining task is not to invent a model; it is to prove that the actual stationary distribution follows the candidate limit. A proof would likely require uniform-in-time control of the centered occupancy process, tightness of the short-queue coordinates, and a Lyapunov or generator argument ruling out non-fixed stationary subsequential limits.
