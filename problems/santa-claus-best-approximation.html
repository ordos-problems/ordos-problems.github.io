Short Statement

Open Problem

Determine the best polynomial-time approximation ratio for restricted max-min fair allocation, and in particular close the gap between the 4+δ4+\delta4+δ algorithm and the 53/1553/1553/15 configuration-LP existence bound.

Background & Context

The restricted Santa Claus problem has two related but distinct open questions. One asks for the integrality gap of the configuration LP; the other asks for the best polynomial-time approximation algorithm. These are not the same because the strongest LP-gap proofs rely on local-search or hypergraph-matching arguments whose polynomial-time convergence is difficult. The best current polynomial-time guarantee is Cheng and Mao's 4+δ4+\delta4+δ approximation. Their same work improved the LP integrality-gap upper bound to about 3.8083.8083.808; Haxell and Szabo subsequently reached 53/1553/1553/15, showing that better allocations exist relative to the LP than current efficient algorithms can necessarily find. The open problem is to close this constructive-versus-existential gap.

What Is Known

Feige and Asadpour-Feige-Saberi established constant-factor existence results for restricted max-min allocation through configuration-LP and hypergraph-matching methods. Annamalai, Kalaitzis, and Svensson gave a polynomial-time 131313-approximation, introducing lazy updates and other devices to make local search terminate efficiently. Cheng and Mao subsequently improved polynomial-time guarantees, first to 6+δ6+\delta6+δ and then to 4+δ4+\delta4+δ for every fixed δ>0\delta>0δ>0; their 2019 paper also proves the stronger existential configuration-LP integrality-gap upper bound 3+21/26≈3.8083+21/26\approx3.8083+21/26≈3.808. The lower-bound side includes the basic hardness barrier of factor 222 for related max-min allocation formulations and the known configuration-LP lower bound of 222. The remaining open problem is the constructive threshold: whether the 4+δ4+\delta4+δ algorithm can be improved, whether the newer 53/1553/1553/15 existence bound can be made algorithmic, or whether a new hardness or algorithmic barrier separates efficient algorithms from the LP gap. Haxell and Szabo (SODA 2023; Combinatorica 2025), Theorem 1.1, improve the restricted configuration-LP gap to 53/1553/1553/15. This is existential, not a polynomial-time allocation algorithm.

The algorithmic history tracks the effort to make hypergraph-matching-style existence proofs efficient. Purely combinatorial local search brought the first usable polynomial constants, and Cheng-Mao's refinements reduced the ratio to 4+δ4+\delta4+δ. The remaining gap to the 53/1553/1553/15 integrality bound may be an artifact of analysis, a real barrier for known local-search algorithms, or a sign that a different rounding method is needed. Any improvement must preserve polynomial running time, not just show that a good allocation exists.
