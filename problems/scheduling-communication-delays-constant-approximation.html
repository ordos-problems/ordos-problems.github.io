Short Statement

Open Problem

Find a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation for P∣prec,c∣Cmax⁡P\mid prec,c\mid C_{\max}P∣prec,c∣Cmax​ with uniform communication delay, or prove that no such approximation exists under a standard complexity assumption.

Background & Context

Communication-delay scheduling models parallel computation and distributed production systems in which a successor task can start quickly if it stays on the same processor but must wait if intermediate data, materials, or setup information crosses processors. This is a natural extension of precedence-constrained scheduling, but the delay constraint couples assignment and sequencing in a way that breaks many classical list-scheduling arguments. The problem appears in longstanding scheduling open-problem lists, including the Schuurman-Woeginger list and later updates discussed by Bansal and subsequent approximation papers. It is attractive for the ORdos collection because the model is extremely crisp: a precedence DAG, identical machines, and one scalar delay. The difficulty lies not in modeling but in finding an approximation method that avoids paying a factor growing with ccc or mmm.

Statement & Model

An instance consists of jobs JJJ with processing times, mmm identical parallel machines, a precedence DAG on JJJ, and a uniform communication delay c≥0c\ge 0c≥0. A nonpreemptive schedule assigns each job to a machine and start time. If i≺ji\prec ji≺j and i,ji,ji,j are scheduled on the same machine, then jjj may start after iii completes; if they are scheduled on different machines, then jjj may start only at least ccc time units after iii completes. The objective is to minimize Cmax⁡C_{\max}Cmax​. The open problem is whether there is a polynomial-time O(1)O(1)O(1)-approximation for P∣prec,c∣Cmax⁡P\mid prec,c\mid C_{\max}P∣prec,c∣Cmax​, where the approximation factor is independent of mmm, nnn, and ccc. A stronger resolution would identify the correct asymptotic approximability, perhaps matching a hardness threshold.

What Is Known

Graham-style list scheduling gives factors that grow with the communication delay. Davies, Kulkarni, Rothvoss, Tarnawski, and Zhang gave a polynomial-time O(log⁡c⋅log⁡m)O(\log c\cdot \log m)O(logc⋅logm) approximation using Sherali-Adams liftings and randomized clustering, a major improvement over delay-linear bounds but still not constant. For non-uniform communication delays, Davies, Kulkarni, Rothvoss, Sandeep, Tarnawski, and Zhang proved logarithmic hardness under standard quasi-polynomial-time assumptions, showing that the broader delay model cannot have the same constant-factor behavior. Kulkarni, Li, Tarnawski, and Ye developed hierarchy-based algorithms for related precedence and communication-constrained settings. As of the 2026 check, the uniform-delay constant-factor question remains the clean unresolved target.

Remaining open problem. Find a polynomial-time constant-factor approximation for P∣prec,c∣Cmax⁡P\mid prec,c\mid C_{\max}P∣prec,c∣Cmax​ with uniform communication delay, or prove that no such approximation exists under a standard complexity assumption.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The best source-checked guarantee here is polylogarithmic in ccc and mmm for uniform delays. Non-uniform delays have logarithmic hardness, so the uniform case is the meaningful remaining constant-factor target. O(log c log m) approximation Non-uniform delay hardness

The central line of progress uses lift-and-round methods: a Sherali-Adams solution induces a semimetric over jobs, and randomized clustering turns fractional precedence/assignment information into a schedule. This yields O(log⁡clog⁡m)O(\log c\log m)O(logclogm), which is qualitatively different from old delay-linear guarantees but still falls short of the open constant factor. The non-uniform-delay hardness paper clarifies that arbitrary edge delays are substantially harder and introduces UMPS as a hardness engine. That negative result sharpens the uniform-delay problem rather than resolving it: the remaining question is whether a single common delay contains enough structure for constant approximation, or whether a different hardness construction can survive the uniformity constraint.
