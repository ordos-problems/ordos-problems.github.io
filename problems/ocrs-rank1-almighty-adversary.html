Short Statement

Distinguish the optimal greedy rank-1 guarantee from the broader almighty-adversary online-rule question.

Open Problem

For the broader online-rule question, specify the adversary's randomness visibility and determine the optimal constant beyond the greedy class. The greedy-class optimum is already known.

Background & Context

Rank-one contention resolution asks for per-element conditional selection guarantees, rather than only expected welfare. Arrival-order information and visibility of random choices affect the model. Greedy OCRSs preselect a downward-closed feasibility subfamily and are robust to almighty ordering; their optimum need not equal that of all online rules.

Statement & Model

Fix a rank-1 matroid on ground set NNN, so the online algorithm may select at most one active element. Given an ex-ante feasible vector x∈[0,1]Nx\in[0,1]^Nx∈[0,1]N with ∑ixi≤1\sum_i x_i\le 1∑i​xi​≤1, each element iii is active independently with probability xix_ixi​. An online contention resolution scheme (OCRS) observes active elements in an order chosen by an adversary and must irrevocably decide whether to select each active element, selecting at most one. In the almighty-adversary model, the adversary sees the entire realized active set before choosing the order. A ccc-selectable OCRS must select every element iii with probability at least cxic x_icxi​, or equivalently select it with conditional probability at least ccc given that it is active. Livanos' greedy OCRS below gives a stronger guarantee than the historical 1/41/41/4. Its optimality theorem is restricted to greedy schemes; it must not be interpreted as a lower bound against every history-dependent randomized online rule. Any broader formulation must additionally specify whether the adversary sees the algorithm's random seed or only the active set.

What Is Known

Livanos, 'Simple and Optimal Greedy Online Contention Resolution Schemes' (NeurIPS 2022), Theorem 1.1, gives a 1/e1/e1/e-selectable greedy OCRS for rank one, improving 1/41/41/4. Theorem 1.3 makes this optimal among greedy OCRSs. These schemes tolerate an almighty adversary; the upper bound is not stated for all history-dependent OCRSs. The familiar 1/21/21/2 result in weaker order models must not be transferred to this model without proof.
