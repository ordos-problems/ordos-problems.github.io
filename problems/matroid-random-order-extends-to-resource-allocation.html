Short Statement

Does the optimal (1−1/e)(1-1/e)(1−1/e) random-order matroid CRS guarantee extend from matroids to the abstract online resource-allocation model used in stochastic allocation and NRM?

Open Problem

If the answer is no, determine the best universal constant and identify the structural property separating matroid-like instances from harder resource-allocation instances.

Background & Context

Random-order contention resolution is one of the places where matroid structure gives an unusually clean constant. In a matroid, an ex-ante feasible fractional vector can be rounded online while preserving feasibility as elements arrive. Lee and Singla prove an optimal 1/21/21/2 OCRS for adversarial order and an optimal (1−1/e)(1-1/e)(1−1/e) random-order contention-resolution scheme for matroids. This constant is tight already for rank one, so the matroid theory is, in that sense, complete. Online resource allocation is broader: a request can consume a vector of resources, may have several feasible assignment options, and the feasible family is not necessarily a matroid even though it is still described by a clean ex-ante relaxation. Will Ma's notes isolate an abstract online resource-allocation model that includes several NRM and stochastic matching formulations. The open problem asks whether the random-order 1−1/e1-1/e1−1/e phenomenon is really a matroid artifact or a more general consequence of IID/random arrivals plus ex-ante feasibility.

Statement & Model

Let an online resource-allocation instance have a finite horizon TTT, request types arriving either IID from a known distribution or as a uniformly random permutation of a known multiset, and for each realized request type a finite set of feasible actions. Taking an action yields reward and consumes a vector of resources with capacities B1,…,BdB_1,\ldots,B_dB1​,…,Bd​; the online policy observes the request and must choose an action or reject irrevocably while keeping realized resource consumption feasible. Let FLU\mathrm{FLU}FLU be the natural ex-ante/fluid LP that fractionally assigns type-action pairs subject to expected capacity constraints and obtains the corresponding expected reward. In the special case where the feasible accepted sets form a matroid, Lee and Singla's random-order contention-resolution scheme gives the optimal guarantee 1−1/e1-1/e1−1/e relative to the ex-ante benchmark. The open problem asks whether every instance in the broader abstract online resource-allocation model admits an online policy with expected reward at least (1−1/e)FLU(1-1/e)\mathrm{FLU}(1−1/e)FLU under IID or random arrival order. If the answer is no, determine the best universal constant and identify the structural property separating matroid-like instances from harder resource-allocation instances.

What Is Known

Kleinberg and Weinberg introduced matroid prophet inequalities with constant guarantees, and OCRSs were later developed as a general online rounding framework for stochastic selection problems. Lee and Singla designed optimal OCRSs via ex-ante prophet inequalities: an optimal 1/21/21/2 OCRS for adversarial-order matroids and an optimal (1−1/e)(1-1/e)(1−1/e) random-order contention-resolution scheme for matroids. The 1−1/e1-1/e1−1/e constant is best possible even for rank one, so the matroid random-order theory is tight. Online stochastic knapsack and multi-unit prophet problems show that LP-relative constants can be characterized for some resource-allocation subcases, but those models do not cover arbitrary type-action systems with multiple resources and non-matroid feasibility. No source checked here proves or refutes the full extension from random-order matroids to Will Ma's abstract resource-allocation model.

Remaining open problem. If the answer is no, determine the best universal constant and identify the structural property separating matroid-like instances from harder resource-allocation instances.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The matroid version is tight at 1−1/e1-1/e1−1/e, and the adversarial-order matroid OCRS is tight at 1/21/21/2. No published result found extends the random-order 1−1/e1-1/e1−1/e guarantee to the full abstract resource-allocation model. Lee and Singla, optimal OCRSs via ex-ante prophet inequalities

The strongest existing theorem is matroid-specific. Lee and Singla reduce optimal OCRSs to ex-ante prophet inequalities and obtain the optimal 1/21/21/2 adversarial-order matroid OCRS plus the optimal (1−1/e)(1-1/e)(1−1/e) random-order CRS. The proof exploits matroid exchange and rank structure. In broader online resource allocation, ex-ante LPs are still central, but feasibility comes from capacities and assignment options rather than a hereditary matroid family. Single-resource stochastic knapsack results show that even one-dimensional capacity can have tight constants below 1−1/e1-1/e1−1/e in adversarial order; random/IID order may help, but it is unclear whether it restores the matroid constant. The open problem is therefore structural: either find a resource-allocation CRS with the same correlation-gap-style behavior, or construct a non-matroid allocation instance where the fluid LP overstates what random-order online policies can achieve.
