Short Statement

Determine the true approximability and relaxation gap of weighted tree augmentation after the recent break below the factor-2 barrier.

Open Problem

What is the optimal polynomial-time approximation ratio, and the right LP/extended-relaxation gap, for weighted tree augmentation?

Background & Context

Tree augmentation is one of the most elementary survivable-network-design problems. Starting from a tree, the goal is to buy extra links so that no single tree edge failure disconnects the network. This is a stylized but precise model for adding redundancy to communication, transportation, and infrastructure backbones. Its appeal is that the base graph is only a tree, so the connectivity requirement reduces to covering a laminar family of cuts. Despite this simplicity, weighted tree augmentation has driven a long line of approximation and LP-rounding work. The old open problem was to beat factor 222 in the weighted case; that has now been solved. The current repository-worthy question is the next, sharper one: after local search and strong LP relaxations pushed the ratio to around 1.51.51.5 and below, what is the actual barrier?

What Is Known

The standard cut LP and several classical primal-dual or relative-greedy techniques give factor-222 guarantees, and for a long time no better approximation was known for weighted tree augmentation. Kortsarz and Nutov emphasized that weighted TAP was the simplest connectivity network-design problem for which a ratio better than 222 was unknown, while the unweighted case had better guarantees. Traub and Zenklusen broke the weighted factor-222 barrier, first obtaining a 1+ln⁡2+ε1+\ln2+\varepsilon1+ln2+ε approximation and then a (1.5+ε)(1.5+\varepsilon)(1.5+ε) local-search guarantee. Their work also connected weighted tree augmentation to broader weighted connectivity augmentation. In 2026, Cohen-Addad, Drygala, Klein, and Svensson proposed a strong LP relaxation with subset variables and a randomized rounding algorithm below 1.491.491.49. The classical cut LP has gap at least 1.51.51.5, so new relaxations are necessary for LP-based progress below that value. The exact approximation threshold, best possible relaxation gap, and matching hardness remain unknown.

The evolution of weighted tree augmentation is a useful warning against preserving stale open problems. Before 2021, the right headline was whether any algorithm could beat 222 in the weighted setting.
