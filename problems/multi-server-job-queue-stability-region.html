Short Statement

A general saturation formula now describes stability beyond phase-type service. The remaining challenge is computing its threshold with certified accuracy and useful complexity guarantees.

Open Problem

Compute the FCFS saturation growth constant with certified error and explicit complexity bounds for a specified finite input representation. A general threshold representation now exists; compact or polynomial-time evaluation, and critical-load behavior, are separate remaining questions.

Background & Context

Modern cloud, parallel-computing, and machine-learning systems routinely run jobs that need several servers or accelerators at once. This destroys the separability of classical many-server queues: a job may be delayed not because total work is too high, but because the wrong combination of servers is available. Under FCFS, large jobs can block smaller jobs behind them, leaving capacity idle while work waits. The resulting stability problem is a clean stochastic-process question with direct operational meaning: it asks when the queue does not explode. It is not a modeling problem; the FCFS multi-server-job model is fixed and simple to state. The difficulty is that the usual traffic condition ∑kλkbkESk<N\sum_k\lambda_k b_k\mathbb{E}S_k<N∑k​λk​bk​ESk​<N is not sufficient in general because fragmentation and head-of-line blocking can waste service capacity.

What Is Known

Wang, Xie, and Harchol-Balter emphasize that even the exact stability region is very hard, and they derive sufficient conditions for zero queueing in large-system scaling regimes. Grosof, Harchol-Balter, and Scheller-Wolf give a closed-form stability region for the two-class FCFS multiserver-job system with distinct server requirements and exponential service distributions. Grosof, Hong, Harchol-Balter, and Scheller-Wolf (Performance Evaluation 162, 2023, article 102378), Sections 2.2 and 3, describe the saturation-rule criterion for Poisson arrivals with i.i.d. joint server-need/phase-type-duration marks. Appendix C computes the threshold from finite balance equations. Theorem 4.2 further gives mean response time to additive O(1)O(1)O(1) as arrival rate approaches that threshold. Thus exact finite-state characterization extends beyond two classes. This is not a polynomial-in-input-size complexity theorem. Baccelli, Olliaro, Ajmone Marsan, and Marin (arXiv:2601.20653v1, 2026), Theorems 4.2 and 5.1, establish a monotone-separable workload recursion and the pile-growth threshold for general finite-mean service durations, including the iid marked renewal model above. Section 5 explicitly leaves the complexity of evaluating γ\gammaγ open. Algorithm 2 estimates the limit numerically; successive estimates becoming close are not a certified error bound. Likewise, their sub-perfect sampling procedure returns lower approximations, not an observable certificate of an exact stationary sample. Remaining open problem. Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). A 2026 preprint extends the saturation representation to general service durations. Finite-state phase-type methods remain available; certified efficient evaluation of the general pile-growth constant is unresolved. Two-class stability result RESET and MARC (2023), model, saturation criterion, Theorem 4.2 and Appendix C Francois Baccelli, Diletta Olliaro, Marco Ajmone Marsan, Andrea Marin (2026)

The 2023 phase-type analysis and the 2026 workload recursion address different levels of the problem. One supplies a finite CTMC computation in a restricted distributional family; the other supplies a broadly applicable limiting representation. Neither alone proves polynomial complexity in the primitive input. This entry now focuses on making the threshold computable with explicit accuracy and runtime bounds. Independent renewal class streams are not silently treated as an iid marked superposition, and critical-load behavior is kept separate.
