Short Statement

A four-period counterexample disproves a single connected ordering region under nonstationary demand. The remaining question is a useful exact structural description with a quantified number of reorder regions.

Open Problem

Bound the number and describe the structure of optimal ordering intervals and order-up-to targets in the stated finite-horizon capacitated backorder model, or exhibit families requiring unboundedly many intervals. Specify how complexity scales with horizon, capacity, and demand support; the continuous-order conjecture itself is already false.

Background & Context

A fixed charge rewards batching, while a capacity limit prevents inventory from being restored in one large order. Their interaction can make ordering attractive at a higher inventory level but unattractive at a nearby lower one: paying the setup cost today changes how later constrained orders can be used. Consequently, ordinary convexity and a single reorder threshold are insufficient. The state is one-dimensional, so a useful structure theorem would improve both understanding and exact computation. This entry uses the finite-horizon backorder model of the cited counterexample rather than mixing several inventory models. It asks how complex an optimal rule must be after the simplest conjecture has failed.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The continuous-order conjecture is disproved for finite-horizon nonstationary demand. The general exact structural-complexity question remains; the cited example does not resolve stationary infinite-horizon variants. Published EJOR paper (2024)

The earlier entry overstated the scope of the counterexample by placing it inside an infinite-horizon model and allowing lost sales. The published construction instead uses four periods, nonstationary independent demand, complete backorders, and zero lead time. Separating these cases leaves a concrete remaining target: quantify how complicated the reorder regions can become, rather than ask for an undefined 'simple' policy or repropose a conjecture already disproved. Observed rarity of violations is empirical evidence, not an approximation theorem.
