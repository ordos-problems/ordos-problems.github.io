Background & Context

Precedence-constrained scheduling is a classical meeting point of operations research and algorithms. Jobs are all unit length and only their partial order creates difficulty. Two machines admit special polynomial structure; allowing the machine count to vary changes the complexity. For decades, the exact complexity with three machines remained a prominent question from Garey and Johnson's list. Quasi-polynomial approximation schemes improved the optimization picture without settling that exact question. A September 2026 manuscript now claims a polynomial-time exact algorithm for the original three-machine model. The reported bound is enormous, and the proof requires independent scrutiny; the historical question is therefore recorded as solution claimed rather than plainly open.

Statement & Model

An instance consists of a finite set JJJ of unit-processing-time jobs, three identical parallel machines, and a precedence partial order ≺\prec≺ on JJJ. A feasible nonpreemptive schedule assigns each job j∈Jj\in Jj∈J to one of the three machines and to an integer time slot, with at most one job processed on each machine in each slot, and with jjj completed before kkk starts whenever j≺kj\prec kj≺k. The objective is to minimize the makespan Cmax⁡C_{\max}Cmax​, the time at which the last job completes. The original problem asks for the classical decision complexity of this clean fixed-machine model: given TTT, decide whether there is a feasible schedule of makespan at most TTT for P3∣prec,pj=1∣Cmax⁡P3\mid prec,p_j=1\mid C_{\max}P3∣prec,pj​=1∣Cmax​. A September 2026 manuscript claims a polynomial-time exact solution of this model. The claim has not been independently certified in this audit.

What Is Known

Earlier hierarchy-based and combinatorial approximation schemes approximate fixed-machine precedence scheduling in quasi-polynomial time but do not decide exact feasibility. OpenAI, 'A Polynomial-Time Algorithm for Three-Machine Unit-Job Scheduling' (September 24, 2026), Theorem 1.1, claims a uniform deterministic algorithm producing an optimal schedule and deciding deadlines in O((L+2)150020)O((L+2)^{150020})O((L+2)150020) Turing-machine steps, where LLL is the complete binary input length. The input is an arbitrary explicitly listed precedence DAG, with three identical machines and unit, nonpreemptive jobs; there are no release dates or communication delays. The claimed exponent is constant but impractical. This is a claimed full resolution of the original complexity target, not merely an approximation result. This audit checked the theorem and model, but did not independently certify the complete proof or a formalization.
