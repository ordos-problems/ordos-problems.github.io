Short Statement

Prove the large switch-over deterministic-cycle limit for branching-type polling systems with renewal arrivals.

Open Problem

Prove or disprove that cyclic branching-type polling systems with general renewal arrivals have the same large deterministic switch-over-time limit as the corresponding Poisson-arrival systems.

Background & Context

Polling systems model a single server cycling among several queues, with applications in manufacturing, communication systems, maintenance, and traffic control. Many exact polling results rely heavily on Poisson arrivals, because Poisson arrivals interact cleanly with branching-type service disciplines and switch-over periods. Boon, Boxma, and Winands highlighted a clean open problem where the model is already fixed: keep the cyclic polling structure and branching-type disciplines, let deterministic switch-over times become large, and replace Poisson arrivals by general renewal arrivals. For Poisson arrivals, the large switch-over-time limit is well understood and the system behaves like a deterministic fluid cycle plus a simple residual-cycle effect. The conjecture is that the same deterministic-cycle limit should survive renewal arrivals. The challenge is that renewal arrivals break the branching and independence identities used in the Poisson proof.

What Is Known

For branching-type polling systems with Poisson arrivals, Winands proves the large deterministic switch-over-time asymptotics: as switch-over times grow proportionally, waiting times and queue lengths converge after scaling to a deterministic cyclic limit with a residual-cycle component. Boon, Boxma, and Winands identify the renewal-arrival extension as an open problem, because the Poisson proof uses branching and independence identities that are not available for general renewal processes. Approximation work for renewal-arrival polling systems combines light-traffic, heavy-traffic, and interpolation ideas, giving useful formulas but not a rigorous exact large-switch-over limit. The open question is therefore whether renewal variability washes out at the large-switch-over scale, or whether residual-life effects can change the limiting cycle.

Remaining open problem. Prove or disprove that cyclic branching-type polling systems with general renewal arrivals have the same large deterministic switch-over-time limit as the corresponding Poisson-arrival systems.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The Poisson-arrival large switch-over limit is solved. Renewal-arrival approximations exist, but the exact deterministic-cycle limit remains open. On open problems in polling systems Closed-Form Waiting Time Approximations for Polling Systems

The Poisson model benefits from distributional identities that make queue lengths at polling epochs behave like a branching process with immigration. Renewal arrivals remove that exact structure because counts in future intervals depend on the current renewal age. On the other hand, when switch-over times scale like rrr, each cycle contains many renewals, so a law-of-large-numbers fluid picture should dominate local renewal variability. The conjecture is plausible but not automatic: a proof must control renewal ages, visit lengths, and service-generated cycle-length randomness uniformly over the whole cycle. The remaining gap is to replace Poisson branching machinery with renewal-fluid or regenerative arguments strong enough to produce the same deterministic-cycle limit.
