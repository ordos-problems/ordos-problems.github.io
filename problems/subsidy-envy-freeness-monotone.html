Short Statement

For monotone valuations with item marginals at most one, determine the minimum subsidy needed to guarantee an envy-free allocation.

Open Problem

For normalized monotone valuations with unit-bounded marginals, determine the minimum worst-case total subsidy. Is n−1n-1n−1 sufficient, or can one prove a superlinear lower bound?

Background & Context

Indivisible goods often do not admit envy-free allocations, but cash transfers can compensate agents and restore envy-freeness. The subsidy question asks how much money is always sufficient. This is a clean hybrid of fair division and quasi-linear utility: allocate indivisible goods, then add nonnegative payments so every agent prefers its own bundle plus payment. For additive valuations with each item worth at most one dollar marginally, Brustle, Dippel, Narayan, Suzuki, and Vetta proved the striking tight result that total subsidy n−1n-1n−1 suffices. For general monotone valuations with bounded marginals, envy can still be eliminated with bounded subsidy independent of the number of items, but the best known bounds are much larger. The main open question is whether the additive one-dollar-each phenomenon extends to monotone valuations.

What Is Known

Johannes Brustle, Jack Dippel, Vishnu V. Narayan, Mashbat Suzuki, and Adrian Vetta proved the tight additive bound n−1n-1n−1 and a quadratic bound for monotone valuations. The lower bound n−1n-1n−1 comes from one good valued at one by every agent. Max Dupré la Tour and Mashbat Suzuki, 'Subquadratic Subsidies for Nonnegative or Nonpositive Valuations' (arXiv:2609.08272v1), Theorem 1, establish total subsidy at most 52(n−1)3/2log⁡(4n)5\sqrt{2}(n-1)^{3/2}\sqrt{\log(4n)}52​(n−1)3/2log(4n)​ for every nnn. All valuations must be nonnegative on every bundle, or all nonpositive; monotone goods satisfy the first condition. This removes the earlier prime-power restriction on the number of agents. The argument is existential, not a polynomial-time algorithm. Remaining open problem. Does the sharp additive n−1n-1n−1 bound extend to arbitrary monotone valuations? More generally, close the linear-to-subquadratic worst-case gap.
