Short Statement

Characterize which valuation domains admit welfare-optimal dynamic posted prices in combinatorial markets.

Open Problem

Characterize the exact frontier of valuation functions for which optimal dynamic pricing schemes exist in combinatorial markets.

Background & Context

Dynamic posted pricing is a decentralized alternative to direct welfare maximization in markets for indivisible goods. A seller posts prices, buyers arrive sequentially, each buyer chooses a utility-maximizing bundle among remaining items, and prices may be updated after each arrival based on the remaining market. The goal is not revenue learning; it is exact welfare implementation by prices. Unit-demand buyers admit optimal dynamic prices, while coverage valuations can fail to admit them. This creates a clean structural question parallel to the role of gross substitutes in Walrasian equilibrium: which valuation domains are exactly those where dynamic prices can implement an optimal allocation without a central coordinator? Berger, Eden, and Feldman narrow this frontier with positive results for small multi-demand markets and a maximal-domain negative theorem around non-gross-substitutes valuations, but the full characterization remains open.

What Is Known

Cohen-Addad and coauthors showed that unit-demand buyers admit optimal dynamic prices and gave examples, such as coverage valuations, where optimal dynamic prices need not exist. Berger, Eden, and Feldman sharpened the picture. On the positive side, they characterized optimal allocations in multi-demand markets and used this structure to give a polynomial-time optimal dynamic pricing algorithm for up to three multi-demand buyers. On the negative side, they proved a maximal-domain theorem: for every non-gross-substitutes valuation, one can add unit-demand valuations so that the resulting market does not admit optimal dynamic prices. Berczi, Berczi-Kovacs, and Szogi later gave a dual approach, rederived the unit-demand and up-to-three multi-demand results, and proved optimal dynamic prices for bi-demand valuations with an arbitrary number of buyers. These results leave open the full valuation-domain frontier, especially for general multi-demand valuations and for the relationship between gross substitutes and dynamic-price implementability.

Remaining open problem. Characterize the exact frontier of valuation functions for which optimal dynamic pricing schemes exist in combinatorial markets.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Unit-demand markets, up to three multi-demand buyers, and arbitrary many bi-demand buyers are covered. Non-gross-substitutes valuations cannot be included in a maximal universal domain with unit-demand valuations. The exact frontier remains open. On the Power and Limits of Dynamic Pricing in Combinatorial Markets A dual approach for dynamic pricing in multi-demand markets

The problem is structurally close to Walrasian equilibrium theory but not identical. Gross substitutes are the maximal domain for several equilibrium guarantees, yet dynamic prices are sequential and can condition on previous sales, so the domain could be larger or organized differently. Existing positive results exploit special allocation structure in unit-demand, bi-demand, and small multi-demand markets. Existing negative results show that non-gross-substitutes valuations create obstructions once combined with unit-demand buyers. The unresolved part is to turn these pieces into an exact theorem: either a maximal domain, a complete classification for multi-demand valuations, or a sharp obstruction criterion.
