Short Statement

Can polynomial-time algorithms recover the hidden matching in correlated Erdos-Renyi graphs down to the information-theoretic threshold?

Open Problem

Close the computational gap for unseeded correlated Erdos-Renyi graph matching: either design polynomial-time exact/almost-exact recovery algorithms matching the information-theoretic thresholds, or prove a computational barrier under a standard average-case hardness framework.

Background & Context

Graph matching asks whether two observed networks have the same hidden vertex labels. In the correlated Erdos-Renyi model, two graphs are generated from a common random parent graph and then independently subsampled, with one graph's labels permuted. The statistical question is whether the hidden permutation can be recovered exactly or approximately; the algorithmic question is whether this can be done in polynomial time at the same parameter values where recovery is information-theoretically possible. This model captures a clean version of entity resolution, network alignment, and average-case inference. Wu, Xu, and Yu determined sharp reconstruction thresholds in dense regimes and near-sharp thresholds in sparse regimes using information-theoretic and maximum-likelihood arguments. Subsequent work sharpened sparse thresholds and designed polynomial-time algorithms based on trees and chandeliers in some regimes. The central remaining issue is whether efficient algorithms can match the information-theoretic thresholds, or whether there is a genuine statistical-computational gap.

What Is Known

Wu, Xu, and Yu established sharp reconstruction thresholds for dense correlated random graph matching and sparse thresholds up to constant factors; their positive results are information-theoretic and rely on maximum-likelihood-type arguments rather than efficient algorithms. Ding and Du sharpened the positive-overlap recovery threshold in sparse correlated Erdos-Renyi graphs. Wu, Xu, and Yu also characterized testing thresholds for unlabeled graph correlation in dense settings and within constant factors in sparse settings. Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Information-theoretic thresholds are now sharp or nearly sharp in major regimes, and polynomial-time motif/tree algorithms succeed in some parameter ranges. The full efficient recovery threshold for unseeded matching remains unresolved. Settling the Sharp Reconstruction Thresholds of Random Graph Matching Matching recovery threshold for correlated random graphs

The statistical side of correlated graph matching has advanced rapidly. Wu, Xu, and Yu used mutual-information and second-moment methods to locate reconstruction thresholds in dense models and approximate sparse thresholds, while maximum likelihood gives information-theoretic achievability. Ding and Du sharpened sparse recovery thresholds. Algorithmically, seeded matching and motif-counting methods provide efficient procedures in favorable regimes, and chandelier/tree statistics show that local structures carry exploitable correlation. The remaining gap is to determine whether such efficient statistics can be pushed to the full information-theoretic boundary, or whether low-degree or other average-case evidence points to inherent computational hardness.
