Background & Context

Historical context before October 2026: Online Steiner Forest is a canonical online network-design problem: connectivity demands appear over time, and infrastructure must be built irrevocably. The greedy algorithm is almost unavoidable in this setting: connect the new demand as cheaply as possible using the network already bought. Despite its simplicity, its exact performance has resisted analysis for decades. The problem is attractive because the lower bound for every online algorithm is only logarithmic, so greedy might be optimal up to constants. At the same time, greedy decisions can create subtle long-term effects: a path that is locally shortest may or may not build useful infrastructure for future terminal pairs. The gap between the simple algorithm and the optimal online lower bound is therefore a clean test of whether local metric contraction captures the right online network-design structure. Historical context: The online Steiner Forest greedy algorithm has a long history because it is both natural and hard to analyze. The Awerbuch-Azar-Bartal analysis gives a squared-logarithmic upper bound by charging greedy paths across distance scales. The universal lower bound is logarithmic, leaving a single extra logarithmic factor unexplained. Bamas, Drygala, and Maggiori show that this extra factor disappears, up to log⁡log⁡k\log\log kloglogk, on a broad class of instances that captures known hard examples.
