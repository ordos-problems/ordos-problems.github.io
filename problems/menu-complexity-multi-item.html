Background & Context

Myerson's theory makes single-item revenue maximization simple: the optimal mechanism is a posted price (for one buyer) or a virtual-value auction. Multi-item revenue maximization is drastically different. By the taxation principle, any mechanism for a single buyer is equivalent to a menu of (lottery, price) options, and Hart and Nisan showed the revenue-optimal menu can be startlingly complex: even for two independent items, optimal mechanisms may need randomization, and for correlated items the optimal revenue may be infinite while every finite-menu mechanism earns only a negligible fraction — an infinite 'menu-size complexity'. This motivated menu size as a clean complexity measure for mechanisms, quantifying the 'simple versus optimal' tension at the heart of multidimensional mechanism design. Two regimes anchor the theory. First, constant-factor approximation is cheap: Babaioff, Immorlica, Lucier, and Weinberg showed the better of selling separately and grand bundling (a menu of n+1n+1n+1 options) achieves a constant fraction of optimal revenue for an additive buyer over independent items. Second, near-optimal approximation is subtle: Babaioff, Gonczarowski, and Nisan resolved the qualitative question by proving a finite menu of size C(n,ε)C(n,\varepsilon)C(n,ε) always suffices for (1−ε)(1-\varepsilon)(1−ε)-optimal revenue, and Gonczarowski's optimal-transport-duality lower bound of Ω(ε−1/4)\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1/4})Ω(ε−1/4) (two i.i.d. items from nice distributions) shows real growth is necessary. What is not known is the quantitative rate: whether C(n,ε)C(n,\varepsilon)C(n,ε) can be polynomial in nnn for fixed ε\varepsilonε (the known exponential lower bounds only kick in when ε≈1/n\varepsilon\approx 1/nε≈1/n), and even the exact exponent of 1/ε1/\varepsilon1/ε for two items, where the bounds Ω(ε−1/4)\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1/4})Ω(ε−1/4) and O(ε−2)O(\varepsilon^{-2})O(ε−2) leave a polynomial gap. These questions connect to communication complexity, mechanism descriptions in practice, and the buy-many literature.

What Is Known

Foundational negatives: Hart, Nisan (EC 2013 / JET 2019) show optimal mechanisms for two independent items may require randomization, and for correlated items every finite menu can miss almost all revenue (infinite menu-size complexity), so finite-menu guarantees require independence. Constant factors are cheap: Babaioff, Immorlica, Lucier, Weinberg (FOCS 2014) show max(SRev, BRev) — a menu of n+1n+1n+1 entries — is a 666-approximation (constant since improved). Near-optimality: Babaioff, Gonczarowski, Nisan (STOC 2017 / GEB 2022) prove C(n,ε)C(n,\varepsilon)C(n,ε) is finite for every n,εn,\varepsilonn,ε, with upper bounds exponential in nnn, and exponential lower bounds when ε≈1/n\varepsilon\approx 1/nε≈1/n. For two items, Hart-Nisan gave O(ε−4)O(\varepsilon^{-4})O(ε−4); Gonczarowski (STOC 2018) strengthened this to O(ε−2)O(\varepsilon^{-2})O(ε−2) under standard economic assumptions and proved the first lower bound Ω(ε−1/4)\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1/4})Ω(ε−1/4), even for i.i.d. values from nice distributions; as a corollary the deterministic communication complexity of (1−ε)(1-\varepsilon)(1−ε)-optimal two-item mechanisms is Θ(log⁡(1/ε))\Theta(\log(1/\varepsilon))Θ(log(1/ε)). Related strands: buy-many mechanisms have poly(n,1/ε)\mathrm{poly}(n,1/\varepsilon)poly(n,1/ε) menu-size complexity in some regimes (Chawla, Teng, Tzamos, EC 2020), symmetric menu complexity of i.i.d. items is quasi-polynomial (Kothari et al. 2019), and communication/quantum-communication analogues have been studied through ITCS 2025. Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). No movement on the two core quantitative questions since Gonczarowski (STOC 2018): the two-item gap [Ω(ε−1/4),O(ε−2)][\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1/4}), O(\varepsilon^{-2})][Ω(ε−1/4),O(ε−2)] and the poly-vs-exponential growth of C(n,ε)C(n,\varepsilon)C(n,ε) in nnn for fixed ε\varepsilonε both stand as of mid-2026. Activity has shifted to adjacent models: buy-many mechanisms (poly menu-size complexity in restricted settings), symmetric menus for i.i.d. items, and the communication and quantum-communication complexity of auctions (e.g. ITCS 2025), which refine but do not resolve the menu-size questions. The Menu-Size Complexity of Revenue Approximation (Babaioff, Gonczarowski, Nisan, STOC 2017 / GEB 2022) Bounding the Menu-Size of Approximately Optimal Auctions via Optimal-Transport Duality (Gonczarowski, STOC 2018)

The upper-bound technology is 'nudge and round': Babaioff, Gonczarowski, Nisan (STOC 2017 / GEB 2022) discretize the menu of an optimal mechanism, rounding allocations and prices so the buyer's choice changes little and revenue loses only ε⋅Rev\varepsilon\cdot\mathrm{Rev}ε⋅Rev, giving finiteness of C(n,ε)C(n,\varepsilon)C(n,ε) but with bounds exponential in nnn; their lower bounds show exponential size is genuinely necessary when the accuracy target scales as ε∼1/n\varepsilon\sim 1/nε∼1/n. Gonczarowski (STOC 2018) introduced the main lower-bound tool at fixed nnn: the optimal-transport (Daskalakis-Deckelbaum-Tzamos) duality characterizing optimal two-item mechanisms, from which he extracted the Ω(ε−1/4)\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1/4})Ω(ε−1/4) bound and, in the positive direction, an O(ε−2)O(\varepsilon^{-2})O(ε−2) menu under standard assumptions, plus the tight Θ(log⁡(1/ε))\Theta(\log(1/\varepsilon))Θ(log(1/ε)) communication complexity for fixed nnn. Hart and Nisan's JET 2019 paper delimits the whole program: with correlated items no finite menu achieves any constant fraction of optimal revenue, so product distributions are essential. Adjacent lines: Babaioff-Immorlica-Lucier-Weinberg's constant-factor 'SRev vs BRev' result shows tiny menus suffice for constant approximation; Chawla, Teng, Tzamos show buy-many mechanisms — a natural robustness restriction — have polynomial menu-size complexity, suggesting the exponential blowup is tied to exotic one-shot lotteries; Kothari, Mohan, Schvartzman, Singla, Weinberg bound symmetric menu complexity for i.i.d. items at quasi-polynomial. The two-item exponent gap persists because the lower-bound construction exploits only local behavior of the transport dual near a critical region, while the upper bound rounds globally; closing it likely requires either a sharper duality-based construction or a fundamentally better rounding that adapts menu density to the distribution.
