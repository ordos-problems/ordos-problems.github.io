Background & Context

Metric uncapacitated facility location is a foundational clustering and network-design model. Facilities have opening costs, clients must be assigned to open facilities, and connection costs form a metric. The problem is simple to state and has been a testing ground for primal-dual, LP-rounding, greedy, and bifactor approximation techniques. The best known general approximation ratio is Shi Li's 1.4881.4881.488, while the best hardness is 1.4631.4631.463. This narrow numerical gap has persisted for more than a decade. Its appeal is that the remaining problem is not one of modelling or asymptotics: determine the exact polynomial-time approximability threshold for a clean metric optimization problem.

What Is Known

Guha and Khuller established the standard 1.4631.4631.463 approximability lower bound for metric facility location. Algorithmically, a long sequence of primal-dual, local-search, greedy, LP-rounding, and bifactor algorithms reduced the upper bound. Jain, Mahdian, and Saberi developed influential primal-dual and bifactor tradeoff tools; Mahdian, Ye, and Zhang improved the ratio to about 1.521.521.52. Byrka and Aardal then gave a 1.51.51.5 approximation by combining a modified Chudak-Shmoys LP-rounding algorithm with another bifactor algorithm and by touching the Jain-Mahdian-Saberi limit curve for connection-cost-dominated instances. Shi Li improved the guarantee to 1.4881.4881.488 through a randomized scaling/bifactor combination, which remains the best known ratio for the standard centralized metric UFL problem in the sources checked here. Later work improves related variants, such as distributed, multi-level, capacitated, or penalty versions, but no source in this sweep improved the 1.4881.4881.488 versus 1.4631.4631.463 window for ordinary metric UFL.

Facility location approximation is driven by tradeoffs between opening-cost and connection-cost guarantees. A bifactor algorithm with guarantee (λf,λc)(\lambda_f,\lambda_c)(λf​,λc​) controls the facility-opening part and connection part separately, and many of the best single-factor algorithms arise by combining algorithms that perform well in different regions of this two-dimensional cost plane. Byrka and Aardal's 1.51.51.5 result shows the power of this viewpoint: their LP-rounding algorithm touches the known bifactor limit curve in the connection-cost-dominated regime and is balanced with a complementary algorithm. Shi Li's 1.4881.4881.488 algorithm pushes the same broad strategy further using randomized scaling. The remaining gap is small but conceptually important. It is unclear whether the bifactor/LP-rounding framework still has room, or whether a new hardness construction can move the 1.4631.4631.463 barrier upward toward 1.4881.4881.488.
