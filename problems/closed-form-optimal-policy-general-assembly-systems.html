Short Statement

For stochastic assembly systems, the optimal balanced base-stock structure is known under broad conditions; can the time-varying levels be computed explicitly, and does comparable structure survive fixed ordering costs?

Open Problem

Develop an explicit, efficiently computable characterization of the time-varying balanced echelon base-stock levels for arbitrary initial imbalance, and characterize the optimal policy when fixed ordering costs are added to the same stochastic assembly model.

Background & Context

Multi-echelon inventory theory asks how to coordinate replenishment across components, subassemblies, and finished goods when demand is random and lead times are positive. Assembly systems are especially difficult because one missing component can block a finished unit even when other components are abundant. Rosling's classic 1989 paper showed that, under a long-run-balance condition on the initial state, a stochastic assembly system can be transformed into an equivalent serial system, leading to optimal balanced echelon base-stock policies. Chen and Muharremoglu later removed the restrictive initial-condition assumption: from any starting state, the optimal policy remains a balanced echelon base-stock policy, but the base-stock levels evolve dynamically with time and state. This means the qualitative structural question is mostly solved. The remaining research-level gap is more computational and explicit: can the dynamic levels be characterized in a form that is usable without solving a large dynamic program, and what happens once fixed ordering costs or more general assembly topologies destroy the clean serial reduction?

Statement & Model

Consider an assembly system in which MMM components are procured with deterministic lead times, one unit of each component is required to assemble one finished product, demand for the finished product is stochastic over time, holding costs are linear at component or echelon levels, and unmet finished-good demand is backlogged or penalized. In the no-fixed-ordering-cost model, decisions are replenishment quantities for each component at each review epoch, subject to lead times and assembly balance. Rosling's serial reduction and Chen-Muharremoglu's extension imply that the optimal policy is a balanced echelon base-stock policy, possibly with base-stock levels that evolve dynamically from arbitrary imbalanced initial states. The open question is to give a closed-form or polynomial-time characterization of these dynamic levels without solving the full high-dimensional dynamic program, and to determine whether an analogous tractable optimal-policy structure exists when component orders incur fixed costs Kj>0K_j>0Kj​>0 or when the assembly network is generalized beyond the in-tree case.

What Is Known

Clark and Scarf introduced the echelon-stock dynamic-programming structure for serial multi-echelon systems. Rosling proved that, under a long-run balance condition, an assembly system can be remodeled as an equivalent serial system and simple reorder policies are optimal. Chen and Muharremoglu completed the no-fixed-cost structural characterization for arbitrary initial states, showing that the optimal policy remains balanced echelon base-stock with dynamically evolving levels. Thus the broad qualitative structure for the classical in-tree no-fixed-cost model is no longer open. The unresolved part is the explicit computation or closed-form description of the evolving levels and the fixed-ordering-cost extension, where inventory control typically moves toward (s,S)(s,S)(s,S)-type regions and balanced base-stock optimality is not established.

Remaining open problem. Develop an explicit, efficiently computable characterization of the time-varying balanced echelon base-stock levels for arbitrary initial imbalance, and characterize the optimal policy when fixed ordering costs are added to the same stochastic assembly model.

Optimal Policies for Assembly Systems: Completing Rosling's Characterization

The important correction for this record is that the main Rosling initial-imbalance issue has already been structurally resolved. Rosling established the serial-system equivalence under long-run balance and thereby reduced an assembly inventory problem to a tractable echelon base-stock policy. Chen and Muharremoglu showed that arbitrary starting states do not destroy balanced base-stock optimality, but they introduce time-varying levels. The open gap is therefore not whether a balanced policy exists in the no-fixed-cost model; it is whether the dynamic levels admit a parsimonious formula or polynomial-time description and whether similar structure survives fixed costs, where inventory control usually shifts from base-stock to (s,S)(s,S)(s,S)-type policies and coupling across components becomes more delicate.
