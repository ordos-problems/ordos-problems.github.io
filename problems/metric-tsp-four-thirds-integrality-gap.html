Short Statement

For metric TSP, the integrality gap of the subtour-elimination (Held-Karp) LP relaxation is conjectured to be exactly 4/34/34/3. Examples force it to be at least 4/34/34/3, but the best proven upper bound is only barely below 3/23/23/2.

Open Problem

Prove or refute that the integrality gap of the subtour-elimination LP for metric TSP is exactly 4/34/34/3.

Background & Context

The metric traveling-salesman problem is one of the central problems in combinatorial optimization. The subtour-elimination LP, also called the Held-Karp relaxation, is the standard relaxation behind both exact TSP solvers and approximation algorithms. The famous 4/34/34/3 conjecture says that every feasible subtour LP solution can be rounded to a tour of cost at most 4/34/34/3 times the LP value, and this would be tight. For decades the best general upper bound was the 3/23/23/2 Christofides-Serdyukov ratio. Karlin, Klein, and Oveis Gharan finally broke the 3/23/23/2 barrier by an extremely small amount using maximum-entropy tree sampling, but the result is still nowhere near 4/34/34/3. This makes the problem a clean benchmark for whether modern probabilistic rounding can fully capture the structure of the subtour polytope.

What Is Known

Families of metric TSP instances give subtour-LP integrality gap approaching 4/34/34/3, so the conjectured value would be tight. Christofides-Serdyukov gives a 3/23/23/2 tour relative to the optimum, and the same ratio was the best known general upper bound for the subtour LP for roughly forty years. Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The general upper bound is now strictly below 3/23/23/2, but only by a tiny constant. Special half-integral and cycle-cut cases have stronger results, and exact small-instance computations support the conjecture. Karlin, Klein, Oveis Gharan, subtour LP gap below 3/2 Jin, Klein, Williamson, 4/3 for cycle-cut half-integral instances Cook, Hougardy, Petrich, exact gap computations

The main lines of attack are probabilistic tree rounding, structure of subtour extreme points, and reductions to half-integral or graph-like instances. Christofides-style arguments naturally stop at 3/23/23/2 because they build a spanning tree and then pay for a parity-correction matching that can be charged by half the LP. The maximum-entropy approach perturbs this charge in favorable cases and gives the first nonzero improvement, but its constants are far from the 4/34/34/3 target. Half-integral instances remain central because many hard examples and conjectural extremal structures live there, and exact computations suggest that small extreme points do not violate the conjecture. The gap is therefore not a lack of examples; it is a missing structural theorem showing that every subtour solution has enough negative dependence, parity slack, or decomposability to support 4/34/34/3 rounding. A counterexample would have to beat decades of half-integral and graph-metric searches with a rigorously certified LP/tour gap.
