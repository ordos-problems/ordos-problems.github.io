Short Statement

For multi-unit auctions with non-identical regular buyers, sharpen the exact finite-kkk revenue gap between optimal discriminating mechanisms and anonymous pricing/reserves.

Open Problem

Determine the exact finite-kkk price of anonymity for multi-unit auctions and identify which hard distributions determine the constants.

Background & Context

The price of anonymity measures the revenue lost when a seller cannot discriminate among buyers. In Bayesian auction theory, Myerson's optimal auction may use distribution-specific reserve prices and allocation rules. Anonymous pricing is much simpler: post one common price and sell while supply lasts. The question is important both practically and conceptually because anonymous mechanisms are transparent, easy to implement, and often legally or institutionally preferable. For one item, the basic approximation gap is now essentially resolved: anonymous pricing loses a factor about 2.622.622.62 relative to optimal discriminating auctions. Multi-unit environments are subtler. Supply kkk changes the role of market thickness, and the gap between anonymous and discriminating mechanisms can depend on whether one compares exact finite-kkk constants, asymptotic rates, or within-group versus cross-group mechanism classes.

What Is Known

Alaei, Hartline, Niazadeh, Pountourakis, and Yuan showed that anonymous pricing gives an eee-approximation to the ex-ante relaxation for single-item auctions, improving earlier four-approximation bounds for anonymous reserves. Jin, Lu, Qi, Tang, and Xiao settled the single-item anonymous-pricing ratio at about 2.622.622.62 and gave matching lower-bound examples. Jin, Jiang, Lu, and Zhang studied multi-unit revenue gaps among Myerson auctions, sequential posted pricing, anonymous reserves, and anonymous pricing. They proved asymptotically tight 1+Θ(1/k)1+\Theta(1/\sqrt{k})1+Θ(1/k​) gaps within discriminating or anonymous groups and Θ(log⁡k)\Theta(\log k)Θ(logk) gaps across the two groups, depending on which mechanism classes are compared. These results give the right asymptotic scale for several comparisons, but they do not pin down the exact finite-supply constants ρk\rho_kρk​ for anonymous pricing versus the optimal auction.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The single-item constant is tight, and asymptotic multi-unit group gaps are known. Exact finite-supply constants and broader multi-item anonymity losses remain open. Tight single-item anonymous pricing Multi-unit revenue gaps

The literature separates two questions: how well anonymous prices approximate optimal auctions, and how revenue gaps scale among broader mechanism classes. Alaei et al. showed that anonymous pricing is within an eee factor of an ex-ante benchmark for single-item settings. Jin et al. later solved the exact single-item constant at about 2.622.622.62, showing the eee bound was not tight. Multi-unit work reveals that supply can either wash out or amplify anonymity losses depending on the comparison class. In particular, within anonymous mechanisms or within discriminating mechanisms the gap tends to one with growing kkk, while cross-group gaps grow like Θ(log⁡k)\Theta(\log k)Θ(logk). What remains is the exact finite-kkk picture and the extension to richer feasibility constraints.
