Short Statement

Are finite two-player mean-payoff games solvable in polynomial time?

Open Problem

Find a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm in the complete binary encoding length for arbitrary finite mean-payoff games. The reported quasipolynomial bound is partial progress, not a solution of this question.

Background & Context

Mean-payoff games are a basic model of infinite-horizon adversarial control. They sit between graph algorithms, optimization, formal verification, and dynamic programming. In verification, they model quantitative properties of reactive systems. In optimization, they are tied to tropical linear programming and policy-improvement methods. In game theory, they are a finite deterministic analogue of average-payoff stochastic games, with positional optimal strategies but unresolved computational complexity. The problem has the same flavor as several central operations-research algorithmic questions: the model is finite and clean, dynamic-programming and strategy-improvement algorithms work well in many cases, but no polynomial worst-case bound is known. The fact that the decision problem lies in NP∩coNPNP\cap coNPNP∩coNP makes it unlikely to be NP-complete under standard assumptions, yet this membership has not yielded a polynomial algorithm.

What Is Known

Ehrenfeucht and Mycielski proved positional determinacy for mean-payoff games. Zwick and Paterson gave foundational pseudo-polynomial and exponential algorithms and clarified the complexity landscape. The problem is known to be in NP∩coNPNP\cap coNPNP∩coNP and is interreducible with energy games and closely related to parity games. Strategy-improvement and value-iteration algorithms are effective in practice but have no polynomial worst-case guarantee; subexponential algorithms are known for related parity/mean-payoff settings. Allamigeon, Benchimol, Gaubert, and Joswig showed that combinatorial simplex algorithms over tropical geometry can solve mean-payoff games, and that a strongly polynomial combinatorial simplex algorithm for linear programming would imply a strongly polynomial algorithm for mean-payoff games. Despite decades of progress on special graph classes and algorithmic frameworks, the general polynomial-time question remains open. Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). A 2026 manuscript claims deterministic quasipolynomial bit complexity. The polynomial-time target remains open. Combinatorial simplex algorithms and mean-payoff games OpenAI: Deterministic quasipolynomial-time mean-payoff games

Mean-payoff games occupy a persistent complexity gap. Positional determinacy gives a compact certificate of optimal play, and the threshold decision problem has both NP and coNP certificates. Zwick and Paterson established the classical algorithms and complexity framework. Subsequent work has attacked the problem through strategy improvement, energy games, parity-game reductions, and tropical linear programming. The tropical-simplex connection is especially relevant to optimization: progress on strongly polynomial simplex-type algorithms would propagate to mean-payoff games, while hard mean-payoff instances inform the limits of pivoting methods. The remaining gap is conceptual as much as technical: existing algorithms exploit numerical weights or local improvement structure, but no proof shows that the number of improvement steps or the size of a certificate can be bounded polynomially in all instances. The September 2026 manuscript targets binary-weight complexity directly; if correct, it improves the upper bound while leaving a polynomial-versus-quasipolynomial gap.
