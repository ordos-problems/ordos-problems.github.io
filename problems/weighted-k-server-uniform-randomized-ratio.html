Background & Context

The weighted kkk-server problem is a natural variant of the classical kkk-server problem. There are kkk servers with different weights, and moving server iii through distance ddd costs widw_i dwi​d. Even on a uniform metric, where every requested point is equally far from every other point, the weighted problem is much less understood than ordinary paging. A request names a point, and the algorithm must ensure that some server is at that point after the request. The unweighted uniform case has logarithmic randomized behavior, while the weighted uniform case has exponential lower bounds and historically only very large upper bounds. Recent work has finally broken the doubly exponential deterministic barrier for randomized algorithms, and an October 2026 preprint claims to close the remaining exponent-order gap. This makes the problem a clean, well-defined constant-in-nnn but parameterized-in-kkk competitive-analysis question.

Statement & Model

Fix kkk servers with weights w1≤⋯≤wkw_1\le\cdots\le w_kw1​≤⋯≤wk​ on a uniform metric space with sufficiently many points. At each online request, a point is revealed; if no server is currently at the point, the algorithm must move one server there and pays that server's weight times the unit distance. The offline optimum knows the full request sequence. Determine the optimal randomized competitive ratio against an oblivious adversary as a function of kkk, or at least close the asymptotic gap between the best lower and upper bounds. In particular, is the true ratio 2Θ(k)2^{\Theta(k)}2Θ(k), 2Θ(k2)2^{\Theta(k^2)}2Θ(k2), or something else between the known singly exponential lower bound and the previously known exp⁡(O(k2))\exp(O(k^2))exp(O(k2)) randomized upper bound? Hwang (2026), Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4, claims the 2Θ(k)2^{\Theta(k)}2Θ(k) alternative: ordinary weighted k-server is the diagonal-request special case of its generalized model. This records a preprint claim, not an independently certified proof or the exact optimal exponential base.

What Is Known

Fiat and Ricklin introduced weighted server variants, and later work showed that the deterministic behavior can be extremely large. Bansal, Elias, Koumoutsos, and Nederlof gave algorithms for generalized kkk-server in uniform metrics and a doubly exponential deterministic guarantee for weighted uniform metrics. Ayyadevara and Chiplunkar proved that the randomized competitive ratio for weighted kkk-server on uniform metrics is at least exponential in kkk, improving the earlier logarithmic-style lower-bound picture. Ayyadevara, Chiplunkar, and Sharma then proposed a decomposition program that reduced part of the randomized upper-bound challenge to relaxed service-pattern problems. Bijoy, Mondal, and Chiplunkar subsequently gave an exp⁡(O(k2))\exp(O(k^2))exp(O(k2))-competitive randomized algorithm on uniform metrics, the first randomized upper bound beating the doubly exponential deterministic barrier. This left a gap between Ω(2k)\Omega(2^k)Ω(2k) and exp⁡(O(k2))\exp(O(k^2))exp(O(k2)). Hwang's arXiv:2610.37192v1, Theorem 1.3, states E[ALG]≤20kOPT+10kwk\mathbb E[\mathrm{ALG}]\le 20^k\mathrm{OPT}+10^k w_kE[ALG]≤20kOPT+10kwk​ for arbitrary weighted uniform spaces and fixed initial configuration. Corollary 1.4 specializes to ordinary weighted k-server. Combined with the lower bound, this claims exp(Theta(k)) against an oblivious adversary, not a matching exponential base.
