Short Statement

In fully online matching, all vertices arrive online and later expire. Determine the optimal competitive ratio against the maximum matching of the full graph, even for bipartite graphs.

Open Problem

Determine the tight integral and fractional ratios separately; the reviewed bipartite fractional bounds are 0.60.60.6 and 0.61320.61320.6132.

Background & Context

Fully online matching sits between classical one-sided online bipartite matching and genuinely dynamic matching markets. In the Karp-Vazirani-Vazirani model, one side is known in advance and the other side arrives online; in fully online matching, every vertex arrives online and later reaches a deadline. This captures settings such as ride-sharing, kidney exchange, ad allocation with expiring opportunities, and peer-to-peer platforms where participants can be matched after arrival but cannot wait forever. The model is clean because the graph and timing rules are fixed: every edge is revealed once both endpoints have arrived, matching is irrevocable, and an unmatched vertex must be matched or discarded at its deadline. The difficulty is that the algorithm must balance two hazards simultaneously: matching too early can waste future opportunities, while waiting can lose expiring vertices. The exact competitive ratio is unknown even in the bipartite case.

Statement & Model

In the fully online matching model, vertices of a graph G=(V,E)G=(V,E)G=(V,E) arrive one at a time. When a vertex vvv arrives, all edges from vvv to previously arrived vertices are revealed. Each vertex has a deadline after all of its neighbors have arrived. At any time before vvv's deadline, an online algorithm may irrevocably match vvv to any currently unmatched neighbor whose deadline has not passed; if vvv is still unmatched at its deadline, the algorithm must either match it immediately or discard it. The benchmark is a maximum-cardinality matching in the full graph.
