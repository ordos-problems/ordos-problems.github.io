Short Statement

Determine whether Directed Steiner Tree admits a polynomial-time polylogarithmic approximation, or prove a separation between polynomial and quasi-polynomial time.

Open Problem

Give a polynomial-time polylogarithmic approximation for Directed Steiner Tree in general directed graphs, or prove that no such algorithm exists under a standard complexity assumption.

Background & Context

Directed Steiner Tree is one of the basic survivable/network-design problems where directionality changes the approximation landscape completely. Undirected Steiner Tree has constant-factor approximations, while the directed version must choose a rooted arborescence through an asymmetric graph and can encode Set Cover-like structure. The model is clean and operationally meaningful: build the cheapest directed distribution, communication, or dependency network from a source to required sinks. For decades the field has had a striking time-approximation tradeoff. Quasi-polynomial algorithms can achieve polylogarithmic ratios essentially matching known quasi-polynomial hardness, but polynomial-time algorithms are stuck at kεk^\varepsilonkε. This makes the problem a benchmark for whether lift-and-round, height-reduction, or recursive-density methods can be made efficient without losing the polylogarithmic guarantee.

What Is Known

Charikar, Chekuri, Cheung, Dai, Goel, Guha, and Li gave the classical quasi-polynomial framework for Directed Steiner Tree. Later work clarified and improved the ratio: Grandoni, Laekhanukit, and Li obtained an O(log⁡2k/log⁡log⁡k)O(\log^2 k/\log\log k)O(log2k/loglogk) approximation in quasi-polynomial time and showed tightness for quasi-polynomial algorithms by tuning Halperin-Krauthgamer-type hardness. Independently, Ghuge and Nagarajan obtained matching quasi-polynomial guarantees through a broader submodular tree-orienteering framework. In polynomial time, the best general guarantee remains O(kε)O(k^\varepsilon)O(kε) for every fixed ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0. Planar digraphs are a major positive special case: Friggstad and Mousavi obtained a polynomial-time O(log⁡k)O(\log k)O(logk) approximation, and Chekuri, Jain, Kulkarni, Zheng, and Zhu extended tree-embedding and LP ideas to planar directed polymatroid variants. These special cases do not resolve the arbitrary directed-graph problem. A 2024 arXiv attempt claiming a polynomial-time polylogarithmic result for general DST was later marked on arXiv as withdrawn/proof-collapsed, so it should not be treated as a resolution.

Planar directed Steiner generalizations Tight quasi-polynomial algorithm

Directed Steiner Tree has a clean divide between what is possible with quasi-polynomial recursion and what is known in polynomial time. Height-reduction and recursive greedy ideas can reduce the instance to bounded-depth structures, but preserving enough terminal coverage information creates quasi-polynomial state spaces. Sherali-Adams and label-consistent subtree formulations sharpen this into the current O(log⁡2k/log⁡log⁡k)O(\log^2 k/\log\log k)O(log2k/loglogk) quasi-polynomial guarantee. Polynomial time loses this structure and falls back to kεk^\varepsilonkε ratios. Planar directed graphs show that graph structure can collapse the difficulty through separators and embeddings, suggesting that the general obstacle is not merely directedness but the absence of compressible decomposition. A resolution likely needs either a new polynomial-size relaxation/rounding framework or a complexity result separating polynomial from quasi-polynomial approximation power for this rooted network-design problem.
