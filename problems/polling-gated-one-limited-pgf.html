Short Statement

In the two-queue polling system where one queue has gated service and the other has one-limited service, determine the joint queue-length probability generating function at polling instants.

Open Problem

Determine the joint queue-length probability generating function, or an equivalent exact transform characterization, for the two-queue polling system with one gated queue and one one-limited queue.

Background & Context

Polling systems are queueing networks in which one server visits several queues cyclically. They model communication protocols, manufacturing cells, traffic signals, and service systems. Many polling disciplines are exactly solvable because their queue-length transforms satisfy branching-type recursions or boundary-value problems. The mixed gated/one-limited two-queue system is striking because it is small, concrete, and still unresolved. Queue 1 uses gated service, so all customers present at the start of a visit are served; queue 2 uses one-limited service, so at most one customer is served per visit. Boon, Boxma, and Winands isolate this as an open problem: the functional equation for the joint queue-length probability generating function is known, but standard substitutions do not close it. This is a crisp applied-probability target, not a modelling exercise.

Statement & Model

Consider a stable two-queue cyclic polling system with one server. Customers arrive to queue iii according to independent Poisson processes with rates λi\lambda_iλi​, service times at queue iii have known distributions with finite means, and switch-over times between queues are included as in the standard polling formulation. The server alternates between queue 1 and queue 2. Queue 1 uses gated service: during a visit, the server serves exactly those queue-1 customers present when the visit began, while arrivals during the visit wait for the next cycle. Queue 2 uses one-limited service: during a visit, the server serves at most one queue-2 customer, if any are present. Let (N1,N2)(N_1,N_2)(N1​,N2​) be the stationary queue-length vector at an agreed embedded polling epoch, and let P(z1,z2)=E[z1N1z2N2]P(z_1,z_2)=\mathbb E[z_1^{N_1}z_2^{N_2}]P(z1​,z2​)=E[z1N1​​z2N2​​] be its joint probability generating function. The open problem is to obtain an exact expression, boundary-value characterization, or other complete transform solution for this joint PGF and the associated stationary queue-length or waiting-time transforms.

What Is Known

Branching-type polling systems and several mixed two-queue disciplines have exact transform solutions. Classical polling theory gives product-form or recursive PGF equations for gated, exhaustive, globally gated, and other branching-type disciplines. Boon, Boxma, and Winands explain that the corresponding exhaustive/one-limited two-queue model can be handled, but the gated/one-limited case resists the usual approach even though gated and exhaustive service often have comparable analytic complexity. They derive the relevant functional equation and show why the natural substitution that solves the exhaustive/one-limited case does not close the unknown transform. As of their open-problems paper and the source sweep here, the exact joint PGF for the gated/one-limited case remains open.

Remaining open problem. Determine the joint queue-length probability generating function, or an equivalent exact transform characterization, for the two-queue polling system with one gated queue and one one-limited queue.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Open). The functional equation and related solvable models are known, but no exact joint transform for the gated/one-limited system is known in the cited open-problems paper. Boon, Boxma, Winands, On open problems in polling systems

Polling-system analysis often succeeds when the service discipline has a branching property: each customer present at a visit beginning can be replaced by an independent offspring population generated during service. Gated service has this property, but one-limited service does not fit the same branching template because a visit removes at most one customer regardless of queue length. The mixed gated/one-limited system therefore has just enough structure to write a functional equation, but not enough for the usual recursive PGF closure. Boon, Boxma, and Winands present it as a benchmark open problem because it is minimal, concrete, and analytically resistant. A solution may require a boundary-value problem on the bidisk, a kernel-canceling substitution not yet identified, or a regenerative representation that bypasses the missing branching property.
