Background & Context

Network revenue management is one of the standard places where prophet-inequality ideas enter operations research. A firm has several reusable or consumable resources, customer types arrive online, and accepting a request consumes a vector of capacities. The usual fluid relaxation is tractable and often gives the right first-order benchmark, but online rounding loses value because accepting one customer can block future customers that need overlapping resources. The LLL-sparse case is a clean structural restriction: every product uses at most LLL resources. It interpolates between single-resource stochastic knapsack and genuinely networked allocation, while still retaining a simple combinatorial parameter. In adversarial order, the canonical guarantee scales like 1/(1+L)1/(1+L)1/(1+L), and examples show that dependence on LLL is not an artifact when capacities are small. On the other hand, large-capacity asymptotics are much more favorable. Amil, Makhdoumi, and Wei connect multi-item order fulfillment and network revenue management to prophet inequalities and prove asymptotic optimality once the minimum starting capacity is large. The open problem is to bridge these regimes with a single bound depending on both sparsity LLL and minimum capacity kkk.

What Is Known

For adversarial arrival order and small capacities, a 1/(1+L)1/(1+L)1/(1+L)-type guarantee is known from the prophet-inequality/online-rounding analysis in Will Ma's notes, and a folklore upper bound of order 2/(1+L)2/(1+L)2/(1+L) indicates that linear dependence on LLL cannot be avoided at unit capacity. Amil, Makhdoumi, and Wei prove asymptotically optimal guarantees for related multi-item order-fulfillment and network revenue-management models when the retailer has sufficiently large inventory; their SSRN abstract states that the method gives new asymptotically optimal bounds for NRM. The open gap is non-asymptotic: no sharp finite-capacity expression is known that smoothly connects the 1/(1+L)1/(1+L)1/(1+L) regime with the asymptotic ratio 111.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The endpoint regimes are understood: a sparse adversarial-order guarantee exists at the 1/(1+L)1/(1+L)1/(1+L) scale, and large-capacity asymptotic optimality is known. The missing result is a finite-capacity guarantee with explicit dependence on both LLL and kkk. Amil, Makhdoumi, Wei, multi-item order fulfillment and NRM asymptotics

This problem sits between single-resource online stochastic knapsack and high-dimensional online resource allocation. Classical NRM policies often analyze asymptotic regimes in which capacities scale, while prophet-inequality and OCRS methods give non-asymptotic constants relative to fluid relaxations. The LLL-sparse model is attractive because the resource interaction graph is limited by support size rather than by the number of resources. Existing guarantees establish the two natural endpoints: for small capacities, online rounding loses a factor depending linearly on LLL; for large capacities, Amil, Makhdoumi, and Wei's method-based formulation and prophet-inequality connection yields asymptotic optimality for multi-item fulfillment and new NRM bounds. The missing theorem would likely require a capacity-aware contention-resolution scheme that tracks not only whether a resource is scarce but how many sparse conflicts remain.
