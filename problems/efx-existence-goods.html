Short Statement

Complete EFX allocations are known for three additive agents. Four agents are the first unrestricted case, with new positive results for small numbers of goods.

Open Problem

Does every instance with four or more agents and nonnegative additive valuations admit a complete EFX allocation, with no bound on the number of goods?

Background & Context

Indivisibility prevents ordinary envy-freeness even in simple examples: if two agents both value the only good, one must receive nothing. EFX relaxes envy-freeness by testing envy after a hypothetical removal from the other agent's bundle. It is stronger than EF1, which permits choosing a favorable item to remove. The precise treatment of zero-valued goods is stated in the model below. For nonnegative additive valuations, complete EFX existence is known for three agents. Four agents remain the first unrestricted case, despite positive theorems for few valuation types and small numbers of goods. Allowing unallocated goods, weakening the fairness inequality, or leaving the additive valuation class changes the question. These distinctions make EFX a useful test of which fairness guarantees can be achieved without disposal or transfers.

What Is Known

Bhaskar Ray Chaudhury, Jugal Garg, and Kurt Mehlhorn establish complete EFX existence for three additive agents. Ben Berger, Avi Cohen, Michal Feldman, and Amos Fiat establish four-agent EFX with at most one good left unallocated. Vishwa Prakash HV, Pratik Ghosal, Prajakta Nimbhorkar, and Nithin Varma extend complete existence to any number of agents with at most three distinct additive valuation functions (EC 2025, arXiv:2410.13580v3). Eyad Alkassar, Mahmoud Fouz, and Kurt Mehlhorn, 'Complete EFX Allocations Exist for Four Additive Agents and Up to Nine Goods' (arXiv:2608.08590v1), Theorem 1, prove complete EFX0 for four agents and at most nine goods, including zero values. The proof is computer-assisted; this audit checked its statement and model, not the certificate corpus. Remaining open problem. Complete EFX for unrestricted four-agent additive instances, and hence the general additive existence question, is not settled by these bounded-size results. Counterexamples for nonadditive submodular valuations do not refute additive existence. Approximate EFX, EF2X, and allocations with unassigned goods are different relaxations.
