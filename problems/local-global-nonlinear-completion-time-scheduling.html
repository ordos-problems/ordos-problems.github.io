Background & Context

The classical single-machine problem 1∣∣∑jwjCj1||\sum_j w_j C_j1∣∣∑j​wj​Cj​ is solved exactly by Smith's rule: order jobs by decreasing wj/pjw_j/p_jwj​/pj​. This is one of the cleanest results in deterministic scheduling. Bansal, Duerr, Thang, and Vasquez study the natural nonlinear extension 1∣∣∑jwjCjβ1||\sum_j w_j C_j^\beta1∣∣∑j​wj​Cjβ​, where β>0\beta>0β>0 is a fixed constant. This objective appears when marginal delay costs are nonlinear, for example through learning effects, convex lateness penalties, or power-like delay disutility. The difficulty is not that the model is vague; the model is completely specified. The difficulty is that for β≠1\beta\ne 1β=1, a pairwise preference between two jobs can depend on the time at which the pair is scheduled and on other jobs placed before them. Their paper develops dominance relations and a local-global conjecture suggesting that certain local ordering constraints may characterize global optimality. The open problem is to settle this structural conjecture and, more broadly, the computational complexity of the nonlinear problem.

What Is Known

For β=1\beta=1β=1, Smith's rule optimally orders jobs by decreasing wj/pjw_j/p_jwj​/pj​. Bansal, Duerr, Thang, and Vasquez show that for β≠1\beta\ne 1β=1 the usual pairwise interchange argument breaks: the preferred order of two jobs may depend on their start time and on intervening jobs. They derive new dominance properties and use them to greatly accelerate exact A* search, but they do not settle the complexity of the problem. Their abstract explicitly states that the complexity status is open for β≠1\beta\ne 1β=1. The local-global conjecture captures the hope that enough local dominance constraints might still force global optimality, but no proof or counterexample is known from the checked source trail. Thus the remaining gap is very concrete: either turn the local structure into a proof and algorithm, or show by counterexample or reduction that nonlinear power objectives fundamentally break Smith-like orderability.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). Smith's rule solves the linear case. The nonlinear case has dominance rules and fast exact-search pruning, but neither a polynomial-time algorithm nor NP-hardness proof is known from the source-checked literature. The local-global conjecture for scheduling with non-linear cost

This problem is appealing for the repository because the model is parsimonious and the open question is sharply algorithmic. The linear case has a complete one-line priority rule, while the nonlinear power case is still not classified. The Bansal-Duerr-Thang-Vasquez paper gives enough structure to make computational search meaningful: one can encode local dominance rules, run exact dynamic programming or A* on small instances, and look for either a proof pattern or a smallest obstruction. A full solution would clarify whether the success of Smith's rule is a linear-objective accident or the visible part of a broader nonlinear local-global scheduling principle.
