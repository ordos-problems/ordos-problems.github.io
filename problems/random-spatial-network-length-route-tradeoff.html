Short Statement

For networks connecting random points in the plane, determine the optimal tradeoff between total normalized network length and average route excess.

Open Problem

Determine the asymptotically optimal tradeoff between total length and route-length statistic RRR for connected networks over random planar points.

Background & Context

This is a clean applied-probability version of a practical network-design question: how much extra infrastructure is needed to make routes nearly Euclidean? Classical objects such as the minimum spanning tree, Delaunay triangulation, relative neighborhood graph, and other proximity graphs give canonical points on the length-versus-route-quality curve. Aldous and Shun proposed a route-length statistic RRR and studied a one-parameter family of proximity graphs by simulation and theory. The unresolved issue is global optimality. Do proximity graphs, or simple augmentations of them, come close to the best possible tradeoff among all connected spatial networks, or is there a fundamentally different construction? The problem is mathematically precise and does not require inventing a model; the model is random points in the plane, total network length, and shortest-path route excess.

What Is Known

Aldous and Shun analyze classical proximity-graph families and use Monte Carlo evidence to illustrate a tradeoff between normalized network length and their statistic RRR. Minimum spanning trees, Delaunay triangulations, relative neighborhood graphs, and related proximity graphs provide explicit feasible networks with different points on the length-versus-route curve. They explicitly leave open how close this one-parameter family is to the optimum over all possible connected networks. Related Euclidean optimization theory gives laws of large numbers for structures such as MST and TSP, and geometric spanner theory supplies worst-case stretch constructions, but neither line identifies the average-case optimal length-route tradeoff frontier for random point sets.

Remaining open problem. Determine the asymptotically optimal tradeoff between total length and route-length statistic RRR for connected networks over random planar points.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Open). The site has not recorded a later resolution of the full optimal tradeoff question. Known work provides candidate constructions and simulations rather than an optimal frontier. Aldous and Shun, 2010

Aldous and Shun's paper sits between stochastic geometry and network design. Proximity graphs offer tractable candidates with known or estimable length properties, but the global optimization problem is over all connected geometric networks, possibly including Steiner-like features and hierarchical road-like structures. The main gap is a lower-bound theory strong enough to certify a tradeoff curve, matched by explicit constructions. Existing Euclidean functionals such as MST and TSP constants control extreme points, and geometric spanner theory controls worst-case stretch, but neither directly answers the average-pair route-excess objective. A solution likely needs multiscale continuum lower bounds relating edge-length density to detour, together with constructions that balance local connectivity and long-range shortcuts.
