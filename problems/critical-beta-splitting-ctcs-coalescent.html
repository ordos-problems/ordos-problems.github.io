Background & Context

Aldous's beta-splitting family is a fundamental model for random rooted binary trees, motivated in part by phylogenetic tree shapes. The critical beta-splitting case has splitting probabilities proportional to 1/(i(m−i))1/(i(m-i))1/(i(m−i)) for a clade of size mmm, producing unusually balanced logarithmic phenomena and a rich connection between recursive tree structure, Markov chains, and exchangeable partitions. Aldous and Janson developed a continuous-time consistent splitting model CTCS(n)\mathrm{CTCS}(n)CTCS(n) whose finite-nnn trees are consistent under deletion of a uniform leaf. This consistency suggests an infinite exchangeable limit CTCS(∞)\mathrm{CTCS}(\infty)CTCS(∞), analogous in spirit to continuum random tree limits but with different merger/splitting structure. Their later papers formalize exchangeable partition representations and fringe-tree limits. The remaining conceptual question is the exact relationship between this infinite critical splitting object and the beta(2,1)(2,1)(2,1) coalescent.

Statement & Model

In the critical beta-splitting model, a clade of size mmm splits into subclades of sizes iii and m−im-im−i with probability proportional to 1/(i(m−i))1/(i(m-i))1/(i(m−i)), independently down the tree. Aldous and Janson embed this discrete Markov-branching tree in a continuous-time consistent splitting model CTCS(n)\mathrm{CTCS}(n)CTCS(n) with real edge lengths. The family {CTCS(n):n≥2}\{\mathrm{CTCS}(n):n\ge2\}{CTCS(n):n≥2} is consistent under deleting a uniformly random leaf and pruning degree-two vertices, which leads to an infinite exchangeable limit CTCS(∞)\mathrm{CTCS}(\infty)CTCS(∞) represented through nested exchangeable partitions. The open problem is to give a complete probabilistic description of this infinite object and its partition-valued dynamics. In particular, determine whether the genealogy or time-reversal of CTCS(∞)\mathrm{CTCS}(\infty)CTCS(∞) is exactly the beta(2,1)(2,1)(2,1) coalescent, a deterministic time change of that coalescent, a projection or size-biased transform of it, or merely shares some marginal split-size asymptotics.

What Is Known

Aldous and Janson constructed the consistent finite CTCS(n)\mathrm{CTCS}(n)CTCS(n) family, proved detailed results for leaf heights and fringe distributions, and formulated an exchangeable infinite limit. Their Part III paper develops the exchangeable partition representation and fringe tree more explicitly, giving a concrete framework in which finite restrictions of CTCS(∞)\mathrm{CTCS}(\infty)CTCS(∞) can be studied. Part IV gives sharper Mellin analysis of leaf heights and normal/large-deviation estimates. Kolesnik supplied contraction-method proofs for related height central limit theorems. These advances clarify many one-dimensional and fringe statistics, but they do not fully identify the partition dynamics with, or separate it from, the beta(2,1)(2,1)(2,1) coalescent.

The finite critical beta-splitting model is explicit, but its infinite limit is subtle because consistency is expressed through deletion and pruning rather than a simple forward growth rule. The CTCS construction gives a natural continuous-time object, and later exchangeable-partition work makes the limit more concrete. The beta(2,1)(2,1)(2,1) coalescent appears as the plausible coalescent-side analogue, but equality of finite-dimensional transition rates, time changes, and projections must be pinned down. The open problem is a structural identification, not just another asymptotic estimate.
