Background & Context

KLS has been one of the central problems at the interface of probability, convex geometry, and algorithms. The model is clean: isotropic log-concave distributions are the canonical high-dimensional convex distributions, and the question asks for a uniform bottleneck/isoperimetric lower bound. The conjecture matters to operations research and theoretical computer science because isoperimetry controls Markov-chain mixing for sampling from convex bodies and log-concave densities, which in turn underlies randomized algorithms for volume computation, integration, and convex optimization. It also sits in a tight web of asymptotic convex-geometry conjectures, including slicing, thin-shell, concentration for Lipschitz functions, and central limit behavior of random projections. The point of the problem is not to choose a better model of demand, service, or uncertainty; the model is already fixed and canonical. The October 2026 proof claims address precisely the dimension dependence.

What Is Known

Earlier bounds progressed from polynomial losses (Lee and Vempala) to Chen's almost-constant bound, followed by logarithmic improvements.
