Open Problem

Background & Context

Graph cut problems are foundational in combinatorial optimization, operations research, image segmentation, clustering, and network interdiction. Multiway Cut is the natural generalization of sss-ttt cut from two terminals to many terminals. The two-terminal problem is exactly solvable by max-flow/min-cut, while three or more terminals create an approximation problem with rich geometric structure. The CKR relaxation converts cuts into simplex geometry, and much of the modern theory studies how to randomly round points in that simplex while controlling boundary-crossing probabilities. The model is parsimonious and fully specified, but the constants remain unresolved. This makes it a good repository entry: a clean graph optimization problem with a precise numerical approximation gap and machine-checkable finite gap instances.

Statement & Model

In the undirected Multiway Cut problem, the input is an undirected graph G=(V,E)G=(V,E)G=(V,E) with nonnegative edge weights and a set of terminals T={t1,…,tk}T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_k\}T={t1​,…,tk​}. A feasible solution deletes a set of edges whose removal separates every pair of terminals; equivalently, it partitions VVV into kkk parts, each containing exactly one terminal, and pays the total weight of edges crossing between parts. The goal is to minimize this cut cost. The problem is APX-hard for k≥3k\ge3k≥3 and has a canonical geometric LP relaxation due to Calinescu, Karloff, and Rabani, in which vertices are embedded into the kkk-simplex with terminals at simplex corners. The open problem is to determine the exact polynomial-time approximation threshold, or at least close the gap between the best algorithms and the best hardness/integrality-gap constructions for arbitrary kkk.

What Is Known

Dahlhaus et al. established early approximation and hardness results; Calinescu, Karloff, and Rabani introduced the simplex LP. Sharma-Vondrak's 1.29651.29651.2965 algorithm was superseded by Brakensiek, Huang, Potechin, and Zwick (STOC 2026), whose Theorem 1.1 gives 1.27871.27871.2787 for arbitrary terminal count. The central technical object is the simplex relaxation. Algorithms design randomized partitions of the simplex and bound the probability that an edge segment is separated. Lower bounds construct finite simplex subdivisions and admissible labelings whose boundary behavior forces high cut cost. Sharma and Vondrak's descending-threshold framework gave the historical 1.29651.29651.2965 constant, while Angelidakis-Makarychev-Manurangsi and Berczi-Chandrasekaran-Kiraly-Madan push the lower-bound side just above 1.21.21.2. The gap is not merely numerical: upper bounds rely on rounding distributions with global simplex symmetries, while lower bounds require understanding higher-dimensional combinatorial topology. A full solution likely needs either a substantially sharper rounding family or a new integrality/hardness construction approaching the algorithmic constant. Large mixtures of rounding schemes improve the upper bound to 1.27871.27871.2787; a separate new analytic preprint strengthens the lower side. Numerical evidence and conditional hardness must be labeled separately.
