Short Statement

Close the gap between the best approximation algorithms and hardness/integrality lower bounds for complete-graph correlation clustering.

Open Problem

Determine the optimal approximation ratio for complete-graph correlation clustering, or substantially close the current algorithmic/hardness gap.

Background & Context

Correlation clustering is a clean model for clustering from inconsistent pairwise similarity data. It does not require choosing the number of clusters in advance, which is why it has become a useful abstraction in data mining, record linkage, entity resolution, computational biology, and consensus clustering. The complete-graph version is the canonical model: every pair of items is labeled similar or dissimilar, and the algorithm chooses the clustering that violates as few labels as possible. The problem is also a crisp combinatorial optimization question about partition metrics, multicut polytopes, and rounding of strong relaxations. Its practical relevance makes fast algorithms important, but the repository-worthy open problem is the theorem gap: the exact approximation threshold is not known.

Statement & Model

In unweighted complete-graph correlation clustering, the input is a complete graph KnK_nKn​ whose edges are labeled +++ or −-−. A clustering is a partition Π\PiΠ of the vertices. Its disagreement cost is the number of positive edges whose endpoints lie in different parts of Π\PiΠ plus the number of negative edges whose endpoints lie in the same part. The objective is to minimize this disagreement cost, with no prescribed number of clusters. The open problem is to determine the polynomial-time approximability threshold of this problem. Published algorithms give constant factors well below the old factor-222 barrier, with recent cluster-LP/Sherali-Adams methods reaching about 1.437+ε1.437+\varepsilon1.437+ε. Known hardness is far weaker, around 24/2324/2324/23 in the recent cluster-LP work, and the cluster-LP itself has integrality gap at least 4/34/34/3. Close the gap: improve the approximation factor toward the true LP/hardness threshold, strengthen hardness beyond the current small constant, or identify a relaxation whose integrality gap matches the optimal achievable ratio. A July 2026 preprint by Garcia-Soriano and Schohn claims a (1.3865+ε)(1.3865+\varepsilon)(1.3865+ε) algorithm in this same unweighted complete-graph model; the exact threshold remains unresolved.

What Is Known

Bansal, Blum, and Chawla introduced correlation clustering and gave early approximation results. Ailon, Charikar, and Newman developed the influential randomized Pivot algorithm and a 333-approximation framework. Chawla, Makarychev, Schramm, and Yaroslavtsev gave a 2.062.062.06-approximation for complete graphs by rounding the standard LP, nearly matching that LP's integrality gap of 222. Cohen-Addad, Lee, and Newman, followed by Cohen-Addad, Lee, Li, and Newman, broke the factor-222 barrier using Sherali-Adams and preclustering ideas, reaching about 1.731.731.73. Cao, Cohen-Addad, Lee, Li, Newman, and Vogl introduced the cluster LP, gave rounding analyses with factors 1.491.491.49 and computationally certified 1.4371.4371.437, proved a 4/34/34/3 integrality-gap lower bound for the cluster LP, and obtained an NP-hardness ratio of 24/2324/2324/23. Later work made the cluster-LP approach much faster. The central approximation gap remains: 1.4371.4371.437 is far above the 24/2324/2324/23 hardness and above the 4/34/34/3 cluster-LP gap. Garcia-Soriano and Schohn (2026), Theorems 1.1-1.2 and Corollary 1.3, claim a (1.3865+ε)(1.3865+\varepsilon)(1.3865+ε) polynomial-time algorithm. This is a preprint improvement, not independently proof-verified here.

Correlation clustering has seen a sequence of barrier breaks. Pivot-style algorithms gave simple constant factors. The standard LP stalled near its integrality gap of 222, and Sherali-Adams/preclustering methods pushed through that barrier to 1.731.731.73. The cluster LP is the current organizing object: it unifies previous relaxations, gives a much better rounding factor, and has a known 4/34/34/3 integrality-gap lower bound. Yet the algorithmic factor, LP gap, and hardness ratio are all separated. This makes the next target precise: either improve rounding closer to 4/34/34/3, find stronger cluster-LP gap examples, or prove hardness that explains why ratios near 111 are impossible. A 2026 preprint claims improved rounding and approximate separation, narrowing but not closing this gap.
