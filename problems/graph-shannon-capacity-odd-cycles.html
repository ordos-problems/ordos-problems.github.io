Short Statement

Determine the Shannon capacity of basic graphs such as odd cycles beyond C5C_5C5​, especially C7C_7C7​, and clarify the computational complexity of the graph capacity invariant.

Open Problem

Compute Θ(C7)\Theta(C_7)Θ(C7​) and, more generally, determine the algorithmic complexity and approximability of graph Shannon capacity.

Background & Context

Shannon introduced zero-error capacity to capture communication when the receiver must decode with no possibility of confusion. Translating a channel to its confusability graph gives a beautiful combinatorial invariant: independent sets in strong powers of the graph are zero-error codes. The model is exceptionally clean, but the invariant is hard because it is asymptotic over all powers of the graph. Lovasz's solution for the pentagon C5C_5C5​ via the theta function is one of the classic successes of semidefinite optimization in combinatorics; it also highlights the mystery, because the same machinery does not settle even the next odd cycles. The problem belongs on ORdos because it is a crisp optimization limit, tied to SDP bounds, asymptotic spectra, and finite-object certificate search.

What Is Known

Shannon defined the capacity invariant and solved several elementary cases. Lovasz introduced the theta number and proved Θ(C5)=5\Theta(C_5)=\sqrt 5Θ(C5​)=5​, giving an efficiently computable semidefinite upper bound that is tight for perfect graphs and some special nonperfect graphs. Haemers introduced rank-based upper bounds that can beat Lovasz theta on some graphs, and later work developed Schrijver, fractional, projective-rank, and asymptotic-spectrum tools. For odd cycles beyond C5C_5C5​, exact capacities remain unknown. Computational and constructive work has improved lower bounds for C7C_7C7​, C11C_{11}C11​, C13C_{13}C13​, C15C_{15}C15​ and related graphs by finding large independent sets in graph powers. Alon and Lubetzky showed that the sequence α(G⊠k)\alpha(G^{\boxtimes k})α(G⊠k) can have complicated behavior, so no fixed finite prefix of powers can generally approximate capacity within subpolynomial factors. Recent asymptotic-spectrum and graph-limit work provides new language and some improved bounds, but the basic small-instance benchmark Θ(C7)\Theta(C_7)Θ(C7​) remains unresolved. The capacity problem has two faces. For a fixed graph such as C7C_7C7​, progress can come from finite combinatorial objects: larger independent sets in C7⊠kC_7^{\boxtimes k}C7⊠k​ improve lower bounds, while theta/rank/asymptotic-spectrum methods improve upper bounds. For the general computational problem, Alon and Lubetzky show why naive finite-prefix extrapolation cannot work: the independence numbers of powers can behave too irregularly. Recent asymptotic-spectrum work reframes capacity as part of a broader theory of asymptotic invariants, but has not eliminated the gap on the standard odd-cycle benchmarks. A solution for C7C_7C7​ would likely reveal a new certificate type, either a surprisingly structured code or a sharper universal upper-bound method. The recent recursive constructions improve the finite-power lower bounds; their reported exact-arithmetic and Lean certificates were not rerun in this audit.
