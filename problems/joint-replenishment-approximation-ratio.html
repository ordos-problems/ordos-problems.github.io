Short Statement

Determine the best possible approximation ratio for the deterministic Joint Replenishment Problem with arbitrary waiting costs, and close the gap between APX-hardness, LP gaps, and the best known constant algorithms.

Open Problem

The open problem is to determine the optimal polynomial-time approximation ratio for offline JRP, and in particular whether the general arbitrary-waiting-cost problem or the JRP-D special case admits ratios substantially below the current 1.7911.7911.791 and 1.5741.5741.574-type bounds, or whether stronger hardness or LP-gap constructions rule this out.

Background & Context

Joint replenishment is a classical deterministic inventory model. It captures economies of scale in procurement: placing one warehouse order can serve many retailers, but each participating retailer also has its own setup cost. This two-level fixed-charge structure appears in distribution, retail replenishment, spare-parts logistics, and coordinated ordering. The model is simple enough to be a crisp combinatorial optimization problem, yet rich enough that power-of-two policies and natural LP relaxations leave real gaps. For operations management, the approximation ratio matters because it quantifies the worst-case value of coordination beyond simple periodic policies. For approximation algorithms, JRP is a structured network-design and lot-sizing problem with time, deadlines, and nested fixed charges. The remaining gap is not about choosing a model: the model has been standard for decades. The hard part is understanding the exact algorithmic price of two-level aggregation.

What Is Known

For the discrete-time JRP with arbitrary waiting cost functions, Bienkowski, Byrka, Chrobak, Jez, and Sgall improved the best offline approximation from 1.81.81.8 to 1.7911.7911.791. For JRP-D, Bienkowski, Byrka, Chrobak, Dobbs, Nowicki, Sviridenko, Swirszcz, and Young gave a 1.5741.5741.574 approximation and proved LP lower bounds of 1.2071.2071.207 and a computer-assisted 1.2451.2451.245 for the standard relaxation; they also showed APX-hardness, including equal-length demand-period cases with a 1.51.51.5 upper bound and 1.21.21.2 lower bound. Online JRP has separate bounds: the best known upper and lower bounds for arbitrary waiting costs are around 333 and 2.7542.7542.754 for the linear-waiting-cost lower-bound regime, while online JRP-D has optimal competitive ratio 222. In continuous time, Segev recently gave the first quantitative improvements over power-of-two-type guarantees for several long-standing variants. None of these results gives the exact offline approximation threshold for general JRP or JRP-D.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). General offline JRP has a 1.7911.7911.791 approximation; JRP-D has a 1.5741.5741.574 approximation and standard-LP gap at least 1.2451.2451.245. Recent continuous-time work improves several power-of-two-style guarantees but does not close the discrete offline approximation threshold. Better Approximation Bounds for JRP JRP with deadlines

The JRP approximation literature has two parallel tracks: arbitrary waiting costs and deadline-only demand periods. Power-of-two policies and one-warehouse multiretailer approximations give broad constant guarantees, but their constants are not tight. The 2013 and 2015 approximation papers improved the constants and, importantly, began quantifying lower bounds for the standard LP relaxation. The known APX-hardness only rules out a PTAS, while the LP gaps remain far below the best algorithms. Segev's continuous-time work shows that power-of-two barriers can be beaten in related formulations, suggesting that the discrete constants may also be improvable. A full solution would either find a new rounding/charging method below the current constants or construct stronger hardness/gap families explaining why the known algorithms are near-optimal.
