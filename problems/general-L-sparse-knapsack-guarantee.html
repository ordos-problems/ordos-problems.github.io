Short Statement

Background & Context

The most general LLL-sparse online knapsack model allows each accepted request to consume fractional amounts of several resources. This is much harder than unit-demand or binary sparse network revenue-management models, because a request can partially fill several capacities and thereby create many different residual-capacity states. The case L=1L=1L=1 is already the online stochastic knapsack problem: requests arrive online, sizes are realized from known distributions, and the algorithm must decide immediately whether to accept. Jiang, Ma, and Zhang resolved the best possible LP-relative guarantee for this single-resource benchmark by giving a best-fit magician with ratio 1/(3+e−2)≈0.3191/(3+e^{-2})\approx0.3191/(3+e−2)≈0.319 and proving tightness with respect to the standard LP relaxation. The multi-resource L>1L>1L>1 version is a clean next step: the model is formal, the fluid benchmark is standard, and sparsity is the only structural handle.

What Is Known

For L=1L=1L=1, Jiang, Ma, and Zhang give the tight LP-relative guarantee 1/(3+e−2)≈0.3191/(3+e^{-2})\approx0.3191/(3+e−2)≈0.319 for general online stochastic knapsack. Their best-fit magician selects feasible sample paths with the most capacity already used and maintains a utilization invariant; they also prove an upper bound showing the constant is tight for the standard LP. For unit-density and other special cases, better or different constants are known, and multi-resource appointment-scheduling applications can exploit additional structure. For binary or unit-demand sparse allocation models, guarantees depending on sparsity can be obtained by other prophet/OCRS techniques. These results do not cover arbitrary fractional consumption vectors supported on up to LLL resources, where one request may be best-fit for one resource and damaging for another. No positive constant for the full L>1L>1L>1 model is recorded in the current site sources.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The single-resource case is tight at 1/(3+e−2)1/(3+e^{-2})1/(3+e−2). No comparable nonzero guarantee is known for arbitrary fractional LLL-sparse consumptions when L>1L>1L>1. Jiang, Ma, Zhang, tight online stochastic knapsack guarantee

Jiang, Ma, and Zhang show that the single-resource setting has a clean best-fit structure: to preserve enough room for future large items, the policy deliberately fills capacity on selected paths as much as possible, and the tight constant follows from a utilization invariant. Moving to LLL-sparse fractional consumptions breaks the one-dimensional ordering of residual capacity. A request can be best-fit on one resource and worst-fit on another, and scarce resources may be correlated through shared requests. Known sparse NRM guarantees handle binary resource incidence, while general online stochastic knapsack handles fractional sizes for one resource. The open problem asks for a synthesis of these two theories: a multi-dimensional contention-resolution scheme that maintains enough residual slack across all sparse supports to obtain any LP-relative constant.
