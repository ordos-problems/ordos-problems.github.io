Short Statement

For nonuniform Sparsest Cut, the O(log⁡nlog⁡log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n}\log\log n)O(logn​loglogn) algorithm and Ω(log⁡n)\Omega(\sqrt{\log n})Ω(logn​) SDP gap do not establish the computational threshold.

Open Problem

Determine the nonuniform Sparsest Cut approximation threshold, improving the general-demand upper bound or proving matching computational hardness; distinguish the separate uniform-demand question.

Background & Context

Sparsest Cut is a clean mathematical abstraction of partitioning a network while preserving demand pairs. It is also the algorithmic face of a deep metric-embedding question: the flow-cut gap for a demand graph corresponds to how well certain metrics embed into ℓ1\ell_1ℓ1​. Leighton-Rao type methods gave logarithmic approximations through multicommodity flow. The ARV theorem improved this to O(log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n})O(logn​) for uniform demands using a geometric structure theorem for negative-type metrics from semidefinite programming. This was a conceptual breakthrough because it linked graph partitioning to high-dimensional geometry. The Ω(log⁡n)\Omega(\sqrt{\log n})Ω(logn​) lower bound is for nonuniform demands, not a matching uniform ARV gap. A better algorithm would need stronger convex relaxations, hierarchy rounds with new rounding, or a combinatorial method that avoids the known embedding obstruction.

What Is Known

ARV gives O(log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n})O(logn​) approximation for uniform Sparsest Cut and Balanced Separator. Arora, Lee, and Naor obtained related O(log⁡klog⁡log⁡k)O(\sqrt{\log k}\log\log k)O(logk​loglogk) guarantees for general-demand instances supported on kkk demand vertices through embeddings of negative-type metrics. Sherman and Kolmogorov give faster and more parallelizable ways to realize essentially the same O(log⁡n)O(\sqrt{\log n})O(logn​) approximation tradeoff. On the lower-bound side, Khot and Vishnoi refuted the constant-distortion Goemans-Linial conjecture and gave superconstant SDP gaps; Naor and Young later proved an Ω(log⁡n)\Omega(\sqrt{\log n})Ω(logn​) gap for the Goemans-Linial SDP. For general demands these upper and lower bounds leave a log⁡log⁡n\log\log nloglogn factor, and an SDP gap is not hardness against all algorithms. The nonuniform Naor-Young construction does not give the same uniform-demand gap.

Remaining open problem. Determine the nonuniform Sparsest Cut approximation threshold, improving the general-demand upper bound or proving matching computational hardness; distinguish the separate uniform-demand question.
