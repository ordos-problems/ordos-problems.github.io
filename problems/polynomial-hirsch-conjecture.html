Short Statement

Santos disproved the original linear Hirsch bound, but it remains open whether every polytope has graph diameter bounded by a polynomial in its facets and dimension.

Open Problem

Prove a polynomial upper bound on the graph diameter of every convex polytope in terms of facets and dimension, or construct an explicit superpolynomial-diameter family of polytopes that disproves the polynomial Hirsch conjecture.

Background & Context

The graph of a polytope is the state space traversed by simplex-type algorithms: vertices are basic feasible solutions, and edges are pivots. Warren Hirsch conjectured in 1957 that a ddd-dimensional polytope with nnn facets has graph diameter at most n−dn-dn−d. Such a bound would have been a clean geometric explanation for short paths through feasible regions of linear programs. Santos disproved the original conjecture by constructing a 43-dimensional polytope with 86 facets and diameter exceeding n−dn-dn−d. That counterexample is fundamental, but it does not imply long simplex paths of superpolynomial length, nor does it rule out a larger polynomial diameter bound. The polynomial Hirsch conjecture is therefore the key residual geometric question: are all polytope graphs polynomial-diameter objects, or can high-dimensional polyhedral geometry force truly superpolynomial shortest paths?

What Is Known

Santos disproved the original Hirsch conjecture with a 43-dimensional polytope having 86 facets and diameter larger than n−dn-dn−d, and his construction also yields linear-factor violations in related families. Kalai and Kleitman proved the foundational quasipolynomial upper bound for polyhedron diameter; Todd improved the form to approximately (n−d)log⁡2d(n-d)^{\log_2 d}(n−d)log2​d, and later refinements improve exponents in some ranges without reaching polynomial. Polynomial or linear bounds are known for special classes, including low dimension, some 0/1-polytopes, and other structured families. General lower bounds remain only linear or mildly super-Hirsch, far below quasipolynomial. Thus both sides are open: no polynomial upper bound is known, and no superpolynomial lower-bound family is known.

Progress & Literature

Latest progress (Partial progress). The original linear Hirsch conjecture is false. The best general upper bounds remain quasipolynomial/subexponential, while known lower bounds do not approach superpolynomial growth. The polynomial gap remains fully open. A counterexample to the Hirsch conjecture An improved Kalai-Kleitman bound for the diameter of a polyhedron

The linear Hirsch conjecture failed, but known counterexamples are still far from refuting polynomial diameter. The upper-bound side descends from the Kalai-Kleitman recurrence and its improvements: these bounds are dramatically better than exponential but remain quasipolynomial in the relevant parameters. The lower-bound side has not produced a superpolynomial family; known constructions show that the Hirsch constant is false, not that polytope graphs can be extremely long. A solution therefore needs either a new global geometric/combinatorial argument giving polynomial diameter, or a construction method that amplifies local non-Hirsch behavior into genuine superpolynomial diameter while preserving realizability as convex polytopes.
